Momentum Rotation / Tactical Asset Allocation — Backtest Report

Window: 2011-01-01 → 2026-05-26 (15.4y, clears the ≥10y rule) Data: yfinance daily, total-return adjusted (auto_adjust). Monthly/weekly/quarterly rebalance. Cost: 0.10% per round-trip on one-way turnover (pessimistic retail-ETF catch-all). Tax drag NOT modeled. Search: full 216-config grid (the spec's discrete space is small enough to exhaustively enumerate) + 6,000 Optuna trials over expanded continuous ranges (987 distinct configs) for robustness mapping.

VERDICT (read the deflated line, not the top-line Sharpe)

PASS on momentum rotation as a standalone alpha source. The only thing that survives deflation + robustness is plain diversification — a static equal-weight SPY/IEF/GLD sleeve — and that is not rotation.

Three things kill the rotation thesis over this universe/window:

  1. The highest-Sharpe, most-robust config in the entire search holds all 3 assets of Pool C equal-weight and ignores the momentum signal entirely (Sharpe 1.00, maxDD −16.9%, turnover 0.35/yr, DSR ≈ 0.96, Sharpe identical across all rebalance days). Momentum ranking adds nothing on top of it.
  2. The best genuine rotation (Pool A, top-3/8, monthly) has a headline inflated by month-end timing luck. Month-end is the single luckiest of 11 rebalance days tested: shift it ±1–5 days and Sharpe collapses 0.91 → 0.56–0.86 and maxDD blows out −18% → as bad as −36% (worse than SPY's −34%).
  3. Nothing clears the <10% max-DD survival bar. Best genuine rotation: −13% to −18%. The sole config under −10% (−9.6%) is, again, a no-rotation static SPY/IEF/GLD with an absolute-momentum cash toggle — and it pays for it with a 5.7% CAGR.

Where rotation does earn its keep: in a broad pool with chronic laggards. Pool A rotating top-3/8 (Sharpe 0.89) beats holding all 8 equal-weight (Sharpe 0.68) — its real job is avoiding the persistent losers (DBC, EFA, EEM in 2011–2024), not catching leaders. In the tight Pool C of good diversifiers, rotation actively hurts (top-1/top-2 Sharpe 0.70/0.80 vs hold-all 1.00) — no duds to avoid, so concentration only adds risk.

Benchmark gate

Aligned 2011-01-01 → 2026-05-26:

Benchmark CAGR Sharpe maxDD Calmar
SPY buy & hold 14.2% 0.87 −33.7% 0.42
60/40 SPY/IEF 9.6% 0.98 −21.3% 0.45
  • Beat SPY on raw return? No — nothing did. SPY's 14.2% CAGR is untouchable over a US-equity-dominated window.
  • Beat SPY on a risk-adjusted axis? Yes. Pool A, Pool C-rotation and static SPY/IEF/GLD all beat SPY on maxDD + Calmar (and the static sleeve beats it on Sharpe too). Each clears the gate on the "scalable lower-DD compounding" axis: higher Sharpe + ~half the drawdown ⇒ leverage-to-match-DD would top SPY's return. But that gate is cleared by diversification, not by the momentum overlay.
  • Beat 60/40? Roughly a wash. No config dominates 60/40 on all axes. Static SPY/IEF/GLD edges it on Sharpe/DD/Calmar (loses on CAGR); Pool A edges it on CAGR/DD/Calmar (loses on Sharpe). 60/40 remains the simplest thing that's hard to beat.

Survival bar (10y backtest, max DD < 10%)

NOT MET for momentum rotation. Best genuine rotation reaches −13% (Pool C weekly) to −18% (Pool A). A monthly TAA holding equities through 2020 + 2022 structurally cannot stay inside −10%. The only config under −10% is a no-rotation static SPY/IEF/GLD with an absolute-momentum cash toggle (−9.6%, but CAGR just 5.7%).

Honest recommendation

PASS on broad-pool momentum rotation as a standalone edge — it is a lower-return, lower-DD equity substitute that 60/40 or a static diversified sleeve matches without the month-end timing fragility, and it never beats SPY on return.

If you want a deployable artifact from this work, it's the static diversified sleeve, not rotation: equal-weight SPY/IEF/GLD, quarterly or monthly rebalance, abs-momentum cash filter optional. Highest Sharpe in the entire search (1.00), near-zero turnover, timing-robust, survives deflation (DSR 0.96), beats SPY on Sharpe/Calmar/DD and ties 60/40. See deployment_config.md.

ENSEMBLE, don't isolate: the one piece of the Antonacci machinery worth keeping is the absolute-momentum (trend) filter as a crash overlay — it trimmed DD a couple points and is the mechanism behind the sole survival-bar-clearing config. The relative-momentum ranking only helps inside a broad pool with chronic laggards to avoid (Pool A), and hurts in a clean diversifier pool (Pool C). If you deploy Pool A rotation anyway, run it weekly (kills the timing artifact) and size for a realistic −20%+ drawdown, not the −18% headline.
