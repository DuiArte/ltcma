Buffett-style Value Screener

Currency: USD · ratios are currency-neutral; margin of safety uses each stock's local price

A transparent five-pillar screen in the spirit of quality-at-a-fair-price investing: durable ROE, low debt/equity, strong free-cash-flow yield, fat gross margins (a moat proxy) and a positive margin of safety versus a two-stage DCF. Each pillar earns a full mark at ROE 20%, D/E 0, FCF yield 8%, gross margin 50% and +40% margin of safety; the Buffett score is their average (0–100) over the pillars that have data — the /Np tag shows how many. The Tier column flags market-cap size (Mega ≥$200B, Large ≥$10B, Mid ≥$2B). The margin of safety is a rough two-stage DCF and is shown capped at ±100%; it is unreliable for holding companies and for financials (banks, insurers), which also lack a meaningful gross margin and free cash flow and so score on fewer pillars — not directly comparable to industrials. Free Yahoo Finance fundamentals are approximate; educational research, not investment advice.

Methodology — the CFA equity-analysis framework

Each report follows the standard CFA equity-valuation process: quality first, then price.

1. Profitability ratios

Gross, operating and net margins measure how much of revenue survives to each level of the income statement. Return on equity (ROE) and return on assets (ROA) measure how efficiently capital generates profit.

2. DuPont decomposition

ROE is broken into three drivers — ROE = net margin × asset turnover × financial leverage. This shows why a company earns its ROE: fat margins, efficient asset use, or balance-sheet leverage. Leverage-driven ROE is lower quality than margin-driven ROE.

3. Solvency & liquidity

Debt/equity gauges balance-sheet risk; the current and quick ratios gauge the ability to cover short-term obligations.

4. Relative valuation (multiples)

P/E, P/B, P/S and EV/EBITDA price the company against its earnings, book value, sales and cash earnings. They are quick but only meaningful in context — versus the company's history, its sector, and its growth.

5. Absolute valuation

The required return is estimated with the CAPM: r = risk-free rate + β × equity risk premium (here 4.5% + β·4.5%). Sustainable growth is g = ROE × (1 − payout ratio). For dividend payers, the Gordon dividend-discount model values the stock as D₁/(r − g). The justified trailing P/E, payout × (1+g)/(r − g), is the multiple the fundamentals support — comparing it to the actual P/E shows whether the market is optimistic or cautious relative to the fundamentals.

Limitations

Data is from free Yahoo Finance feeds and is approximate; single-stage DDM assumes constant growth, which suits mature firms better than high-growth ones. This is an educational framework, not investment advice.
