Composite Picks

Every numeric variable from both stock pipelines — CFA/Buffett fundamentals (ROE, margins, FCF yield, margin of safety, growth, leverage, 12-1 momentum) and macro-signal sensitivities (earnings yield, β to rates / dollar / CAD, macro R²) — is z-scored across the 40-name universe, then combined into one composite score per ticker, weighted by each variable's correlation with realised forward returns. The variable direction is set by that correlation, not hand-picked. Tickers are ranked by composite. The 20 picks (★) follow a two-sided band at the rank-20 cut: a name already on the list stays while it ranks 25th or better; a name off the list enters as soon as it ranks 15th or better (it takes the place of the worst-ranked pick); any other free place goes to the best-ranked name off the list. So a pick can rank 21-25, and a name ranked 16-20 can wait off the list. Today ORCL is kept by the band. Each row shows the variables that contributed most (direction + relative size); the underlying weights are proprietary. Educational research on the public universe — not advice, and not a statement of any holding.

Methodology & limitations

Composite. For each ticker in the union of the two pipelines, collect every numeric variable, z-score it cross-sectionally (missing → 0), and form a weighted sum. Weights = each variable's Pearson correlation with realised forward returns; the sign of that correlation sets the variable's direction. Normalised so the absolute weights sum to 1. Rank by composite. The 20 picks follow a two-sided band at the rank-20 cut: a name already on the list stays while it ranks 25th or better, a name off the list enters once it ranks 15th or better (displacing the worst-ranked pick), and any other free place goes to the best-ranked name off the list. The band changes only membership, never the composite or the ranks.

Forward-return proxy. The only realised forward-return history available across the universe is the post-FOMC 5-day drift, so weights are derived from each variable's cross-sectional correlation with the mean post-FOMC 5-day return. This is a genuine realised-return signal but a narrow one; a broader forward-return panel would sharpen the weights. If that history were absent the picker falls back to equal-weight z-scores (documented on the build).

Universe. The composite runs on the names that carry both fundamental and macro-signal variables (the macro-signal coverage set). The broader S&P 500 Buffett value screen stays available via the per-company tool.

Pick Tracker. Every build adds the full ranking to data/picks_history.csv (rebuilt once from the page's committed history). Each ranking is dated by the trading session whose closing prices it holds: a build after the close counts for that day, a build after midnight or before the open for the previous session (only when that session has no ranking of its own), and a build during the session is not recorded. A re-run on the same day replaces that day, never duplicates it. Composites are stored at the precision the page publishes. A day the picker could not rebuild the page is a gap: nothing is carried forward. Tenure is counted between rankings that exist: a gap inside a spell does not interrupt it, and an entry or exit next to a gap is dated by the first / last ranking that shows it (the true date lies inside the gap; entries after a gap are marked ~). A partial ranking (fewer names than the universe) keeps its prices, but its top 20 is not used. Splits are applied only when Yahoo confirms them; a split-like jump makes returns across it n/a unless Yahoo's own split record (a last split earlier than the jump) shows it was a real price move.

List membership. The published list is always 20 names. Before the 09 Nov 2026 ranking it was the plain top 20 by composite. From that ranking on, a two-sided band applies at the cut: a name already on the list stays while it ranks 25th or better; a name off the list enters as soon as it ranks 15th or better, and the worst-ranked pick makes room (only a pick ranked 21-25 can be displaced); any other free place goes to the best-ranked name off the list. The previous list is the one stored for the last recorded session before the build's own; if there is none, or it does not hold 20 names, the build falls back to the plain top 20 and the page says so. The band changes only which names carry the star: the composite, the ranks and the weights are the same. 15 and 25 were chosen on a ranks-only replay of the 84 stored rankings from 04 Jun to 03 Nov 2026 (turnover and how often a well-ranked name was kept off), never on returns; the Track Record shows that replay, extended to every ranking since. data/picks_history.csv keeps the raw rank, the top-20 flag and, separately, the published membership, the rule that built it and the date of the macro inputs.

Macro inputs. The composite's macro variables (betas to the 10-year yield, the dollar index and USD/CAD; P/E; the post-FOMC 5-day drift that sets the weights) come from two private files. Rankings before the 09 Nov 2026 ranking used files frozen at May 2026 data (the USD/CAD betas ended 27 Nov 2025); from that ranking on they are rebuilt every Sunday from Yahoo prices (stocks, the dollar index, USD/CAD) and FRED (the 10-year yield; fed funds for the decision labels), and the line under the page title gives the date of their prices and of their regressions. The 09 Nov 2026 ranking on the rebuilt inputs is marked as a method change: names that entered or left there did so at the change, not as ordinary turnover, and the day-to-day move into it is left out of the exit model. FOMC meetings before a stock's first trading day are not counted.

Exit estimate. A pick leaves the list when its rank falls below 25, or, if it ranks 21-25, when a name off the list ranks 15th or better and displaces it. The estimate is empirical and models only the rank exit: a Markov chain over rank bands fitted to all day-to-day moves between full rankings of every name ranked 25 or better (ranks do not depend on membership), so for a pick ranked 21-25 it can overstate the stay. The page shows the median time to first exit and the model's range for it (10th to 90th percentile: 8 in 10 names starting in the band leave inside it); a block bootstrap gives the uncertainty of the median itself. Fewer than 30 moves or 15 days in a band reads "insufficient history", and anything longer than the history itself reads "> N td" (N = trading days tracked so far), because the history cannot date an exit further out than that. Next to the model's chance of leaving within 20 trading days the page shows how often that actually happened in the history (counted over overlapping name-days, so it rests on far fewer independent cases than its count); the chain ignores how sticky a name's band is, so it tends to overstate movement in the middle bands. Ranks are re-derived every build, so a pick can leave in one day when the inputs move.

Overlays. Earnings dates, surprises and estimate revisions, analyst ratings and consensus targets come from Yahoo Finance (aggregates only, fetched every build; a block or outage makes the cell read "not available today", never yesterday's value). Insider trades come from SEC EDGAR Form 4 filings (purchases, code P, and sales, code S, open market or private; non-market purchases excluded; plan sales flagged; names as filed); a new issuer is backfilled over several days and says so until complete. Exxon Mobil's insiders are read from both of its SEC registrants (the holding company that has carried the XOM listing since 1 Jul 2026, and the former company, whose filing list is re-read monthly). Each insider in the detail table gets an own record: how that person's own past trades in the stock (purchases, code P; sales, code S, open market or private) were followed by the stock over the next 60 trading days against SPY (price only, from the trade day's close; a purchase is right when the stock beat SPY, a sale when it lagged). 10b5-1 plan trades are not counted, only trades whose 60-day window has closed count, and the number of independent (non-overlapping) windows comes first: the result is shown as "k of n" only from 3 independent windows, otherwise as too few independent trades. The horizon was fixed before any result was seen. It is descriptive and small-sample (only the Form 4s the site holds count; the insider detail says from when), not a forecast. Congress trades come from the site's STOCK Act dataset (checked for new filings every weekday), counted by the member who filed them, with the owner (self, spouse, joint, dependent child) as filed. News sentiment is not covered. None of the overlays enters the composite or the ranks.
