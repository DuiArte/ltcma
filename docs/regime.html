Stress Vol Multiplier

The composite stress index is the z-scored average of seven inputs spanning four facets (economic, political, AI-cycle, environmental). A reading above +0.5 flags stress; below −0.5 flags calm; in between is neutral. The stress-vol multiplier compares realised S&P monthly vol in the identified stress regimes vs the calm ones — this cross-validates the ≈1.5–1.6× figure used in the LTCMA's regime- switching Monte Carlo.

Facet Breakdown

The composite blends four facets so no single channel dominates. Each reading is the standardised (z-score) average of that facet's inputs — above +0.5σ is elevated (red), below −0.5σ is benign (green). The environmental facet is an energy-price stress proxy (WTI crude volatility): there is no clean, free, long-history climate or disaster series, so it captures the energy-transition / commodity-price channel, not physical climate risk. The AI-cycle facet reads the semiconductor sector's drawdown from its trailing-12-month high as a proxy for the AI investment cycle.

Findings drawn from the companion macro-overlay event-study work (macro_event_study.py). These are short-horizon patterns that sit above the LTCMA's 12-year base case — useful for timing entries and exits inside the strategic allocation, not for resetting it.

Post-FOMC drift

Across 48 FOMC meetings, the S&P 500 drifted an average of +1.4% in the 20 trading days after each meeting — matching the academic Lucca & Moench (2015) finding. Real and tradeable; the equity tilt in the LTCMA's Edge-Tilted book benefits from this on each new meeting cycle.

VIX panic → equity rally

When the VIX closes above 30, the S&P has returned, on average, the dollar amount shown in the chart over the following 21 trading days — the well-known mean-reversion effect. Consistent with this LTCMA's finding that stress regimes amplify volatility without reliably depressing returns.

CPI surprises

178 CPI releases (2012–2026) were bucketed by surprise size. Signal is real but noisier than the FOMC or VIX patterns.

How to read this page

Regime changes do not arrive on a clean date. The right discipline is to watch the smoothed composite (blue line) cross a threshold and stay there for a few months before adjusting strategic exposures. The tactical overlay below is for shorter-horizon tilts inside the strategic book. Definitions in the Glossary.

Methodology & honest data-source attribution
  • S&P 500 historical P/E + EPS growth (1960–2025): Damodaran histimpl.xls, NYU Stern. True annual values.
  • Sector trailing & current P/E (Jan 2026 snapshot): Damodaran pedata.xls, money-making firms only. SPDR ETFs (XLK…XLC) are mapped to a basket of Damodaran industries and averaged. True cross-section.
  • Sector P/E historical series (heatmap, leadership, dispersion): proxy = TTM dividend yield / current Damodaran payout ratio, then anchored so the latest month matches the Jan-2026 trailing P/E. Levels are approximate; relative dynamics across sectors and time are the signal.
  • Sector dividend yield: computed from yfinance daily dividend events / Close, resampled monthly. True data.
  • Excess-return-vs-SPY: Adjusted Close, monthly resample, geometric compounding. True.
  • Sector payout ratio (current): Damodaran divfund.xls. Used as a constant in the P/E proxy — this is the largest single assumption; if payout has drifted materially in a sector, the proxy level for early years is wrong (relative shape less affected).
  • Total-shareholder yield (dividends + buybacks): not included — SPDR ETFs don’t publish buyback yields cleanly. Dividend yield only.
  • XLC inception 2018, XLRE 2015: earlier values are NaN by design, not faked.
  • Backtest-ready file: sector_features_monthly.parquet in Downloads/sector_valuation_regime/ — columns include pe_proxy, pe_proxy_zscore, div_yield, div_yield_zscore, excess_return_12m/36m, plus regime fields pe_leader, pe_leader_duration, pe_dispersion, pe_dispersion_zscore, regime_label.
