Each point is the money-weighted return from the first trade to that day: the one rate at which every buy and sale up to then, and that day’s value, net to zero — expressed over the period, not annualized, as the record is under a year old. The grey lines put the identical cash flows into the S&P 500 on the same dates, so both face exactly the same money. Solid is dollars, dotted the same trades in pesos; the gap between them is the currency, not the investing.

MWRR — +28.56% (USD)

The internal rate of return of every dated trade against today’s value, over the 255 days since the first trade (over the period, not annualized). This is what the money earned, weighted by how much was invested and for how long — no peak, no average, no assumed base. In pesos the same trades read +35.07%.

TWRR — +21.89% (USD)

Daily returns chain-linked with deposits and withdrawals stripped out. This is what the decisions earned, independent of how much money happened to be in at the time. It is the industry standard (GIPS) and the only one of the two that can fairly be set against an index.

Both numbers are true and they are not interchangeable. Money-weighted sits 6.7 points above time-weighted (in dollars): more money was at work while returns were higher, so the sizing and timing of the capital added to what the decisions earned. Quoting either one without saying which it is, is the mistake this panel exists to prevent.

Against the index, both ways: on decisions, year to date, the book returned +21.89% against the S&P 500’s +13.11% from the 31 Dec 2025 close, a +8.77-point difference (in pesos +33.24% against +16.57%). The book bought nothing before 13 Feb 2026: staying out was a decision, so its year counts those weeks at 0% while the index carries whatever it did over them. On money — putting the identical 235 cash movements into SPY on the identical dates — the index would have earned +15.24% money-weighted against the book’s +28.56%, a +13.32-point difference (in pesos +20.80% against +35.07%). The second is the harder test and the one that cannot be flattered by the timing of the flows: both sides receive exactly the same money on exactly the same days.

What this does not prove. 2026-08-05 is the last date on which an independent broker statement confirmed these share counts (20 of 20 matched). Positions after it are walked forward from the transaction blotter, which reconciles to the published book to the centavo — but a trade that never reached the blotter would not show up until a newer statement is loaded. The peso series also carries a small approximation between its endpoints, where the one peso-quoted holding is ramped rather than marked daily; every headline figure above reads an endpoint, where it is exact.
