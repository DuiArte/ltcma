Currencyone switch for the whole page

Pesos by default, the book’s own currency. The switch above (it stays in reach as you scroll) moves every figure and every chart on this page to US dollars and back, one currency at a time; your choice is remembered on this device only.

Display note: figures are scaled by a fixed constant for confidentiality — magnitudes are illustrative, not the real amounts; prices, returns and weights are exact. Positions and cost basis come from the broker statement (source of truth), re-priced live. Accounting: every position is measured against its cost basis — Return = (market value ÷ cost) − 1. For US-dollar holdings the peso P/L is split into Stock (the US price move at the exchange rate we bought at) and FX (the peso's move on the original cost); the two sum to Total P/L = market value − cost. (Stock here folds in a small price×FX interaction term, itemized separately in FX Attribution below.) The MXN money-fund cash sleeve, FX positions and non-custodian bank cash are excluded from the equity book.

MWRR — what the money earned

The internal rate of return of every dated trade against today’s value, over the 273 days since the first trade (over the period, not annualized). It weighs each return by how much was invested and for how long — no peak, no average, no assumed base. Its figure is the MWRR tile above, in the currency the switch shows.

TWRR — what the decisions earned

Daily returns chain-linked with deposits and withdrawals stripped out. This is what the decisions earned, independent of how much money happened to be in at the time. It is the industry standard (GIPS) and the only one of the two that can fairly be set against an index.

Both numbers are true and they are not interchangeable. Money-weighted sits 1.5 points below time-weighted in pesos: more money was at work while returns were lower, so the sizing and timing of the capital cost part of what the decisions earned. Quoting either one without saying which it is, is the mistake this panel exists to prevent.

Against the index, both ways. On decisions, year to date, the vs index tile is the book’s TWRR less the S&P 500 from the 31 Dec 2025 close. The book bought nothing before 13 Feb 2026: staying out was a decision, so its year counts those weeks at 0% while the index carries whatever it did over them. On money — putting the identical 262 cash movements into SPY on the identical dates, the S&P, same money tile — the book’s money-weighted return is 7.81 points above the index’s, in pesos. The second is the harder test and the one that cannot be flattered by the timing of the flows: both sides receive exactly the same money on exactly the same days.

What this does not prove. 2026-08-05 is the last date on which an independent broker statement confirmed these share counts (20 of 20 matched). Positions after it are walked forward from the transaction blotter, which reconciles to the published book to the centavo — but a trade that never reached the blotter would not show up until a newer statement is loaded. The peso series also carries a small approximation between its endpoints, where the one peso-quoted holding is ramped rather than marked daily; every headline figure above reads an endpoint, where it is exact.

When I bought and sold, against the market’s and my book’s peaks

One date axis for what the charts around it show apart: on top, the time-weighted return this year of the book and of the S&P 500 (the two lines of the chart above, flows removed — the decisions, not the deposits); below, the capital deployed, the open cost basis (the same scaled series as the cost-basis line in the next chart); and that chart’s trading days this year — ▲ buys, ▼ sells; on the bottom panel sized by the amount dealt. Each line is shaded from itself up to its own running peak (no shading means a new high), so an entry made deep in a dip or an exit made at a peak shows as a triangle in or out of the shading; the hover gives the fall from that peak in percent. For privacy the marker days carry a small offset (a day or so), so read entry and exit timing to within a couple of trading days; the distances below the peaks are read on the day shown. Hover or tap a day for both lines; a triangle for the trades behind it. One currency at a time: the switch at the top of the page.

Timing read off a chart is a description of what happened, not a test of skill and not a recommendation; past performance is no guide to the future. Money levels carry the page’s display scaling; returns are exact.

FX Attribution

The currency in the returns. Year to date, time-weighted, the book’s return in pesos sits +10.62 points from its return in dollars; for the S&P 500 (SPY × USD/MXN against SPY) the gap is +3.21 points. Money-weighted, since the first trade, it is +4.33 points for the book and +3.87 for the S&P 500 fed the same money. The gap is the peso’s move against the dollar, weighted by when the book held what — which is why every chart on this page shows one currency at a time (the currency switch at the top of the page) instead of both on top of each other. Same daily walk and YTD path as the Performance tiles.
