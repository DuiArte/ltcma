Buy-the-Dip / Sell-the-Rip — Daily-Timeframe Backtest

Universe: 9 ETFs (yfinance, adj. total-return) + Gold/Silver spot (FxPro M5→daily, real spread). Period 2010-01-04 → 2026-05-26. Held-out wall: 2024-12-31 (post-date never used in selection).

Costs: ETFs 0.10% round-trip; metals real per-bar spread + slippage. Sizing: 1% fixed-fractional risk/trade (ratios are scale-invariant). Optuna ~600 IS trials/instrument + 4-fold annual walk-forward.

Verdict

The concept earns its reputation — robust but boring. Buy-the-dip-in-uptrend produces a positive, low-drawdown edge across almost the entire parameter space (80–99% of valid configs have positive Calmar on US equities, developed and metals), so the edge is structural, not a curve-fit artifact. What does NOT survive cleanly is the specific optimized parameter set for the weaker instruments, and the most statistically robust instruments (SPY/QQQ) had a flat held-out 2025+ window while metals/EM (weak stats) happened to shine — a regime inversion, not evidence of a tradable metals edge.

Recommendation: deploy as a diversified ensemble, not a single instrument. Robust survivors that beat S&P on Calmar: EFA, QQQ, SPY. Equal-risk blend @1% risk: CAGR 3.1% / MDD -3.0% / Calmar 1.01 (S&P Calmar 0.36).

The honest apples-to-apples: at a realistic 10% drawdown budget, the ensemble earns ≈10.1% CAGR vs a de-levered S&P's ≈3.6% — same drawdown, ~2.8× the return. It wins decisively on risk-adjusted return and drawdown, not on raw return: matching the S&P's headline 12.2% CAGR would need ~×11 leverage (34% theoretical), which is impractical and unsafe for a retail ETF basket. Treat this as a drawdown-reducing diversifier / S&P-replacement at lower risk, not a return-amplifier. See equity_curves/PORTFOLIO.png.

Honest caveats

  • "Sell the rip" adds nothing. 10/11 selected configs chose long_only; the symmetric short side was optimized away almost everywhere. The edge is buy-the-dip only — shorting rips in downtrends lost money on this universe.

  • Held-out window is short and single-regime (~17 months, 1–13 trades/instrument). The 2025+ inversion (US-equity flat, metals/EM strong) is suggestive, not decisive. Don't read the metals/EM held-out Sharpes as a durable edge — their overfitting stats are weak.

  • Drawdowns are tiny only because risk is 1%/trade and the book is in cash most days. Real deployment requires scaling risk; practical leverage on an ETF basket is ~2–3×, capping realized CAGR in the mid-single digits at a ~10% drawdown.

  • No survivorship/look-ahead in signals (indicators use only past bars; entry is next-bar open), but yfinance adjusted prices bake in dividends — fine for a price strategy, slightly flattering vs trading the raw ETF.

Method notes

  • DSR deflates the in-sample daily Sharpe by the number of valid trials (multiple-testing), using realized skew/kurtosis. It is a probability the true Sharpe>0 after deflation: >0.50 = more likely real than not; >0.90 = strong. With ~530 trials the bar is harsh — only SPY/QQQ clear ~0.8.

  • PBO (CSCV, 16 blocks, full valid-config grid) = probability the in-sample-best config underperforms the OOS median. <0.50 is the pass line; lower is better. (Computing PBO on only the top configs inflates it spuriously — see pbo_topk column in summary.csv for that biased version.)

  • Concept robustness (frac_calmar_pos): fraction of the entire valid parameter space with positive Calmar. High values mean the edge is structural, not a single lucky knob setting.

  • Walk-forward: 4 anchored annual folds (2021-2024), re-optimized each fold with a 10-day embargo.

  • Monte Carlo (100k): IID trade-sequence bootstrap; plus +50% cost/slippage and ±1-day entry-jitter variants.

  • Fills reconstructed pessimistically from daily OHLC: gaps fill at the open, stop wins ties with TP.

Backtests run under a fixed validation protocol on pessimistic retail costs. Verdicts are research findings, not investment advice; past results do not guarantee future outcomes.
