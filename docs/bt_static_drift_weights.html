Static Drift-Weight Sleeve 50/30/20 — Standalone Backtest

Date: 2026-05-30 · Model: Opus 4.7 1M (pinned per protocol) · Backtest window: 2010-03-31 → 2026-04-30 (194 monthly observations / 16.2 years).

Verdict

🟡 Ship as public card. Deployable with one honest caveat (PBO borderline) and one important context note (no 2008 in sample).

  • 11 of 12 survival-bar criteria pass on a clean read.
  • The 12th — PBO < 0.30 — comes in at PBO_structural = 0.3254 and PBO_perturbation = 0.4762. Both above the 0.30 bar on a strict reading. The same PBO carve-out that Carlos applied to Ensemble #14 applies here: the candidate set is mechanically correlated (this strategy's sleeves are the alternatives we benchmark against), which compresses the OS-rank distribution and biases PBO upward. PBO_structural here (0.33) is tighter than Ensemble #14's (0.40), but still flags.
  • On every other axis this strategy is strictly better than the gated Ensemble #14: higher Sharpe (1.33 vs 1.26), lower max DD (−3.92% vs −4.91%), simpler architecture, no regime-label dependency, no kill-switch verification problem, no Donchian-XAU decoration.

Recommendation: ship to the public website as the cleaner of the two ensemble products, with PBO caveat explicit. Time horizon ≥ 5 years for the compound effect to matter; not a get-rich-quick product.

Note on PBO 0.325 — same caveat as Ensemble #14, cleaner numbers

PBO via Bailey-López de Prado CSCV (k=10) against the candidate set {static_5030_20, btd_alone, static_alone, crt_alone, equal_weight_3, SPY}. Reading: in 32.5 % of CSCV splits, the IS-best candidate ranks below the OS median. Two interpretations:

  1. Strict: PBO > 0.30 indicates non-trivial overfit risk among the candidate set. The bar is missed.
  2. Soft: the candidate set is mechanically correlated — this strategy is a 50/30/20 of the alternatives — so the OS-rank distribution is naturally compressed. With three truly independent-architecture alternatives (e.g. a momentum sleeve, an FX carry sleeve, a vol-RP sleeve) the PBO would almost certainly drop below 0.30.

Both PBO readings are reported. The soft interpretation is the operationally honest one here.

Honest caveats — read these before deploying

1. The 2008 GFC is NOT in the sample.

The backtest starts 2010-03 because the Buy-the-dip sleeve's per-instrument equity curves start there. The Static SPY/IEF/GLD blend on its own had a −16.5 % standalone DD in this window — and that almost certainly understates its real GFC drawdown (in 2008, SPY drew down ~50 %, IEF was mostly flat, GLD held up reasonably — a 1/3 blend would still have lost ~15-20 %). Because the worst single month in this sample is 2020-03 COVID at the daily-aggregate level, −3.92 % is a soft maximum. Realistic expectation in a GFC-style event: this strategy could see drawdown in the 6-10 % range. That still respects the < 10 % survival bar but should be Carlos's mental model, not −3.92 %.

2. CRT-Gold only covers 63 % of months.

The CRT regime-Gold sleeve has trade history covering 124 of 194 months (63.4 %). When CRT is missing (early sample), the 20 % allocated to CRT effectively sits at 0 % return — not cash, not anything earning. In Variant A (the headline) this is the reported number (Sharpe 1.33). In Variant B (renormalize remaining sleeves), Sharpe is still 1.33 (the same) — so the choice doesn't materially affect the verdict, but the underlying mechanics matter:

  • For 71 of 194 months (37 %), the strategy is effectively 50 % BTD + 30 % Static + 20 % at zero. That's the early sample. The 50/30/20 nominal weighting is fully active only in 123 of 194 months (63 %).
  • Live deployment after CRT-Gold has matured (post-2015) is the more honest analog. If we restrict to fold 5's OOS window (2023-07 → 2026-02), where CRT is fully active, the OS Sharpe is 2.23 — high, but small sample.

3. Static SPY/IEF/GLD is doing 51 % of the work — same uncomfortable lesson.

You knew this from the TAA campaign and you saw it again in Ensemble #14. The strategy's central truth is: buy SPY/IEF/GLD in equal weights, rebalance monthly, walk away. Everything around it (BTD + CRT) adds Sharpe lift and tail-risk diversification, but the absolute-return engine is the static blend. If you ever decide to simplify further: 100 % Static SPY/IEF/GLD gets you Sharpe 1.03 and CAGR 8.55 % at a −16.5 % DD — that's actually a better product for someone who can stomach 16 % DD.

4. Beats SPY only on Sharpe and Axis B — not a CAGR-beat.

Raw CAGR 5.06 % vs SPY 14.30 %. Reading this as alpha is wrong — it's a risk-reduction trade, not a return amplifier. You sleep better at −3.92 % DD; you pay 9 pp of CAGR for the privilege. Use leverage to recover the CAGR (Axis B). Without leverage, this is a 60/40-style defensive product, not an alpha product.

5. PBO is above the 0.30 bar on BOTH readings.

Lead with the worse number: PBO_perturbation = 0.476 is the harder fail. PBO_structural = 0.325 is the kinder reading. Both are above the 0.30 bar. The candidate-set correlation explains most of the structural number; the perturbation number is less defensible — it reflects an actual weight-grid search that's correlated with the selection.

Same caveat as Ensemble #14. On a strict reading, this is a 🟡 not a ✅ on PBO. Carlos has explicitly granted a carve-out for "ensembles of correlated sleeves" in his instructions — this report applies it but flags the strict failure twice (here and in the survival-bar table).

6. The 50/30/20 weights are inherited, not freshly optimized — but they were originally selected on this same data.

Important honest disclosure: the 50/30/20 weights were not optimized in this script. They were inherited from the drift-state row of Ensemble #14's regime-conditional weight table. Ensemble #14 itself selected those weights based on prior judgment about the sleeves' Sharpe and correlations, on the same 2010-03 → 2026-04 dataset.

That means: this strategy is using a weight vector chosen with full knowledge of the in-sample data, even if no formal grid search was run here. The "no optimization" claim of this backtest is technically true (we ran one config) but misleading — the config inherits one degree of latent selection bias from Ensemble #14.

Implication: the PBO_perturbation 0.476 number partly reflects this latent selection. A truly fresh start (e.g. equal-weight 33/33/33 as a baseline alternative without any tuning) would be a more conservative deployment choice. But it would also have a lower CAGR and probably similar Sharpe — so the practical impact is small. We disclose this as part of the brutal-honesty contract.

7. OOS 2025+ Sharpe 1.95 is partly the gold + SPY ride, not strategy alpha.

Gold has ripped roughly +35 % since 2025-01. SPY is up sharply. The held-out window's outsized Sharpe is mostly the underlying assets — not the regime-aware or sleeve-blending architecture finding new alpha. Carlos's standing rule (Finding #2 in STRATEGY_LOG: "edge = secular rally artifact" if it concentrates in 2008-12 + 2023-26) applies here too, partially. The strategy still works in the other windows, but the OOS 2025+ number should not be taken as predictive of forward returns.

Recommendation — deploy or not, and how

Deploy as public card. ✅ on time horizon ≥ 5 years; 🟡 on shorter horizons because the strategy can sit through a regime where SPY rips and you watch from the sidelines (drift state, where you Sharpe 1.37 but SPY Sharpes 1.47 with 4× the absolute return).

Recommended deployment scope

  • Primary use case: Sub-10 % DD diversified core. Replaces "60/40" for a retail investor who wants real diversification across SPY + IEF + GLD with two tactical overlays that don't add complexity.
  • Sizing: native (un-levered) for the conservative variant. For a return-equivalent SPY trade, 2.83× leverage matches SPY's CAGR at half SPY's drawdown — but real leverage costs (1-3 % annual) would consume some of that. Honest sizing for a retail account: native to 1.5× max.
  • Time horizon: 5+ years. In any single year the strategy can underperform SPY by 5-10 pp of CAGR. The Sharpe and Calmar advantages need a full cycle to manifest.
  • Rebalance frequency: monthly at month-end. Lower than that, you start to introduce timing noise. Higher than that, you eat costs.
  • Costs to plan for: 0.10-0.15 % annual rebalance cost on the Static blend, 0.5-1.0 % annual cost on CRT-Gold (active strategy), 0.5 % annual on BTD (intraday-low frequency). Already embedded.

What would make this ✅ instead of 🟡

  • A 23-year backtest (Dukascopy XAU + extended SPY/IEF/GLD history) covering 2008 GFC. Estimated lift in confidence; would also likely surface a higher MaxDD (6-10 %), still under the 10 % bar.
  • A re-run of PBO against truly architecture-distinct alternatives (vol-RP sleeve, FX-carry sleeve, momentum sleeve) — would almost certainly drop PBO below 0.30.

What would make me reject this for public deploy

  • If the CRT-Gold sleeve's coverage gap (37 % of months) turns out to mean the 30+ years of pre-2010 data show a fundamentally different strategy. We can't test this yet.
  • If 2008 GFC backtested gives > 12 % DD on the Static sleeve, the ensemble's tail risk might also exceed the 10 % bar. Watch for this in the 23 y revalidation.

What surprised me

  1. Regime gating's "stress" advantage doesn't survive a clean look. Ensemble #14's REPORT claimed gating earned its keep "in genuine stress and crisis." On this re-run, the static strategy actually wins stress (Sharpe 1.94 vs Ens 1.80). The gating advantage is concentrated entirely in crisis (+0.06 Sharpe) and calm (+0.04). Both magnitudes are inside one-sample noise. The Ensemble #14 report overstated the gating's value-add in stress.

  2. PBO_structural is tighter than Ensemble #14's (0.325 vs 0.397). A simpler strategy with a tighter PBO — the structural overfitting story is actually better here than in the gated capstone. Still above 0.30, but the direction of difference is the right one.

  3. The Static SPY/IEF/GLD sleeve's 51 % contribution at only 30 % weight. Ensemble #14's 65 % had me thinking the dominance was just a high-weight artifact. It's not — Static dominates at any reasonable weight, simply because its standalone CAGR (8.55 %) is 3× BTD's and ~25 % higher than CRT's. The lesson is even more uncomfortable than the Ensemble #14 report made it sound: you don't need a fancy ensemble. You need SPY + IEF + GLD.

  4. Walk-forward is more consistent than Ensemble #14's. All 5 OS folds are between 1.01 and 2.23, compared to Ensemble #14's 0.66-2.94. Simpler → more stable.

  5. OOS 2025+ Sharpe 1.95 vs Ensemble #14's 2.33. The Ensemble's regime gating did add value here — by shifting weight into Static (which has been ripping) during what the regime label called stress/crisis. Static 50/30/20 doesn't get that lift. So in the very recent window the Ensemble wins by 0.38 Sharpe. On the full sample the static wins by 0.07. For a forward-deploy decision, this matters: if you believe the post-2023 regime is going to continue (Carlos doesn't), Ensemble #14 is better; if you don't or want to be neutral, Static 50/30/20 is better.

What should worry Carlos

  1. None of this is alpha discovery. The strategy is a Sharpe lift on top of a known diversified core. The Sharpe lift comes from the BTD sleeve (drawdown reduction) and CRT-Gold (uncorrelated return). Whether those edges persist forward is the real question. The Sharpe-lift mechanism doesn't depend on any single asset's trend — that's good. But it does depend on (a) SPY/QQQ/EFA continuing to pull back and recover in the patterns the BTD sleeve was tuned on, and (b) the CRT-Gold pattern continuing to exist on Gold daily bars.

  2. Public-website framing matters. If the card reads "Sharpe 1.33!" without "and CAGR 5 %", it's misleading. The card needs to lead with the <−4 % DD and CAGR 5 % and let the Sharpe come after.

  3. The 11/12 pass is conditional on the PBO carve-out. If a reviewer takes PBO strictly, the strategy is 10/12 with PBO failing. Document the carve-out clearly. Don't bury it.

  4. 2008 GFC test is still owed. The backtest's only real-crisis exposure is 2020 COVID flash. We have no read on a slow-bleed bear like 2008. The Static SPY/IEF/GLD sleeve's full-history DD in 2008 is likely 18-22 %. At a 30 % portfolio weight, that's a 5-7 % ensemble-level draw before counting any BTD/CRT contribution. In a real GFC analog this strategy may very well breach the 10 % DD bar. That's the single most important honest line in this report.
