Summary

  • US large cap median 6.3 (n=7, range 4.9-7.6) trails global equity at 6.8 (n=5) and EM equity at 8.1 (n=6). Managers broadly expect non-US equities to outpace the US.
  • In bonds, intermediate Treasuries sit at 4.8 (n=6) against cash at 3.7 (n=5) and US Agg at 4.4 (n=5). IG at 5.3 (n=4) and high yield at 5.7 (n=6) leave thin credit premia.
  • The widest disagreements are US small cap (IQR 231bp, n=5), commodities (IQR 230bp, range 2.3-6.7, n=6), private equity (range 6.6-10.5, n=5) and private credit (range 5.0-9.2, n=4).
  • Private equity's median of 9.7 (n=5) is the highest on the table. The illiquidity premium drives the top of the range, while AQR's fee-adjusted levered DDM sits at the 6.6 low end.
  • This site's LTCMA is furthest from the public view on thin rows: below the UK equity median of 8.9 (n=2), and above the single-publisher gold and long Treasury figures.
  • Among rows with at least four publishers, this site's LTCMA sits below the commodities (4.5) and high-yield (5.7) medians and below cash (3.7), and above the EM USD sovereign median of 5.6 (n=7).
  • Prediction markets price a near-certain hold at the next FOMC with a small hike tail, and essentially no cuts this year. That contradicts the easing paths assumed by Goldman Sachs AM, UBS, Mercer and State Street.
  • Markets put recession odds in the low single digits, in line with the soft-landing majority and against PIMCO's call that a credit-loss cycle is starting. Democrats are favored for the House and, less strongly, the Senate.

Material divergences

Rows where the publishers materially disagree: public IQR >= 150bp with n >= 4, or >= 1 overweight and >= 1 underweight stance. Arguments are paraphrased from the publishers' own documents.

US small cap — Small-cap estimates span 4.5-7.5, the widest IQR among US equities; stances often contradict the numbers.

both divergence · public IQR 231bp, n=5 · stances ▲1 ■0 ▼2
Higher-return / positive side: Goldman Sachs AM, AQR, BNY Investments
Goldman Sachs AM sees attractive valuations, rate cuts, accelerating earnings and a revival in dealmaking. AQR's and BNY's model outputs sit at the top of the range, although BNY pairs its high number with an underweight because it expects a muted size premium.
Leans on: Starting valuations, an assumed Fed easing path, earnings acceleration, AQR's real return plus inflation build, and BNY's factor model.
Lower-return / negative side: Invesco, J.P. Morgan AM, T. Rowe Price
Invesco, at the 4.5 low end, says weak long-term earnings growth caps returns despite smaller valuation drag. J.P. Morgan AM says weaker profitability offsets cheapness. T. Rowe Price sees small caps only roughly in line with large caps.
Leans on: Long-run earnings growth, profitability and margin gaps against large caps, and valuation-drag assumptions.
What would settle it: Small-cap versus large-cap earnings growth, the share of unprofitable small caps, and whether Fed cuts arrive; markets currently price none this year.

China equity — Rests on two numeric estimates, 5.8 and 8.7; stances split on earnings and policy.

directional divergence · public IQR 145bp, n=2 · stances ▲1 ■0 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: T. Rowe Price, Goldman Sachs AM
T. Rowe Price, at the 8.7 top, expects a high return from an EM earnings rebound despite the highest equity volatility. Goldman Sachs AM is overweight on attractive valuations and light positioning, but says the case depends on earnings delivery.
Leans on: Starting valuations, investor positioning and a projected EM earnings recovery.
Lower-return / negative side: Lazard, Invesco, J.P. Morgan AM
Lazard says China's apparent stability hides structural weaknesses that need large government intervention. Invesco, at 5.8, relies on a currency tailwind to offset weak earnings growth and a negative buyback yield. J.P. Morgan AM flags high volatility and policy uncertainty.
Leans on: Earnings growth, net buyback/dilution yield, the currency forecast and policy-risk assessments.
What would settle it: Chinese earnings revisions, net share issuance versus buybacks, the scale of fiscal stimulus, and the CNY/USD trend.

US IG corporate — IG range of 4.1-5.5: carry at elevated yields versus spreads near the tight end of history.

directional divergence · public IQR 50bp, n=4 · stances ▲1 ■3 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: Invesco, Goldman Sachs AM
Invesco, at the 5.5 top, says a total yield above Treasuries supports returns with limited credit losses. Goldman Sachs AM cites mid-cycle fundamentals, value in segments such as banks, and rate cuts lifting total returns.
Leans on: Starting total yield, low expected credit losses and an assumed rate-cut path.
Lower-return / negative side: Vanguard, T. Rowe Price, PIMCO, Mercer
Vanguard is underweight because rich valuations give little compensation for credit risk. T. Rowe Price, at the 4.1 low end, assumes tight starting spreads followed by modest widening. PIMCO and Mercer are neutral, saying tight spreads limit upside; PIMCO prefers moving up in quality.
Leans on: Starting spread levels against history, assumed spread widening, and scrutiny of structure-reliant ratings.
What would settle it: The IG option-adjusted spread path from current tights, the downgrade rate, and the level of Treasury yields.

US high yield — High yield: carry and strong credit metrics versus PIMCO's call that a credit-loss cycle is starting.

directional divergence · public IQR 92bp, n=6 · stances ▲2 ■1 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: Goldman Sachs AM, T. Rowe Price, Invesco
Goldman Sachs AM cites strong credit metrics, contained defaults, a better ratings mix and supportive M&A. T. Rowe Price expects carry to outweigh spread widening. Invesco, at the 6.1 top, starts from a high total yield net of expected losses.
Leans on: Starting total yield, default trends, ratings-mix improvement and M&A activity.
Lower-return / negative side: PIMCO, State Street, BNY Investments
PIMCO is underweight with high conviction, saying tight spreads reflect complacency as losses begin, especially in lower-quality, levered credit. State Street, at the 4.6 low end, sees only a modest premium over IG. BNY expects spreads to widen toward their long-run median.
Leans on: Default probabilities, the spread term structure, leverage in lower-quality issuers, and spread mean reversion.
What would settle it: Default and distressed ratios, recovery rates, and HY spreads versus their long-run median, especially in CCC and highly levered issuers.

Commodities — Range of 2.3-6.7 reflects methodology more than a cycle view.

numeric divergence · public IQR 230bp, n=6 · stances ▲1 ■1 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: AQR, Robeco, T. Rowe Price
AQR, at the 6.7 top, applies the long-run excess return of a diversified futures basket on top of real cash. Robeco expects geopolitical supply disruptions and dollar dynamics to support returns. T. Rowe Price sees a moderate return with equity-like volatility.
Leans on: A very long history of futures excess returns, real cash yields, supply-disruption risk and the dollar path.
Lower-return / negative side: BNY Investments, State Street, Invesco
BNY, at the 2.3 low end, keeps a low expected return roughly unchanged. State Street builds its estimate from inflation and dollar forecasts, long-term roll return and collateral yield. Invesco values commodities as a diversifier with its lowest expected return.
Leans on: Inflation and USD forecasts, long-term roll return assumptions and collateral (T-bill) return.
What would settle it: Futures curve shape (roll yield), the T-bill collateral rate, the USD trend, and realized supply disruptions.

Bank loans — Rest on two numeric estimates; the split is rate relief versus leveraged-lending losses.

directional divergence · public IQR 45bp, n=2 · stances ▲1 ■0 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: Goldman Sachs AM, Invesco, T. Rowe Price
Goldman Sachs AM expects lower rates to ease interest burdens and improve issuers' coverage. Invesco and T. Rowe Price point to high floating-rate carry with little duration; T. Rowe Price ranks loans highest among public US fixed income.
Leans on: Floating-rate yield, an assumed path of falling policy rates, and interest coverage ratios.
Lower-return / negative side: PIMCO
PIMCO is underweight and expects significantly higher losses in leveraged lending, where floating-rate structures and high leverage raise default risk.
Leans on: Issuer leverage, floating-rate debt-service burdens and expected loss rates.
What would settle it: Loan default and recovery rates and interest coverage. Markets pricing no cuts this year challenges the rate-relief channel.

Private equity — PE range of 6.6-10.5: illiquidity premium versus fees, leverage cost and elevated valuations.

numeric divergence · public IQR 155bp, n=5 · stances ▲3 ■1 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: BNY Investments, T. Rowe Price, Fidelity International
BNY, at the 10.5 top, raised its estimate sharply on the illiquidity premium and private capital's role in funding AI and infrastructure. T. Rowe Price and Fidelity International, at the 9.7 median, also assume an illiquidity premium over public equity net of fees, and stress manager selection.
Leans on: An illiquidity premium over public equity, structural demand for private capital, and fee assumptions.
Lower-return / negative side: AQR, Goldman Sachs AM, State Street
AQR, at the 6.6 low end, uses a levered dividend discount model net of heavy fees. Goldman Sachs AM is neutral, seeing elevated valuations and range-bound multiples. State Street builds from leverage-adjusted listed-equity history net of fees.
Leans on: Public equity valuations, debt cost, fee drag and leverage-adjusted historical returns.
What would settle it: Exit and distribution activity, entry multiples against public equities, and leveraged financing costs.

Private credit — Range of 5.0-9.2: an illiquidity premium versus early stress in direct lending.

directional divergence · public IQR 143bp, n=4 · stances ▲2 ■2 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: T. Rowe Price, Goldman Sachs AM
T. Rowe Price, at the 9.2 top, cites the illiquidity premium and structural demand for private capital. Goldman Sachs AM notes yields above public markets with lower historical defaults, and favors mezzanine, asset-backed finance, Asia direct lending and secondaries.
Leans on: Yield pickup over public credit, historical default rates and structural demand for private financing.
Lower-return / negative side: PIMCO, AQR, BNY Investments
PIMCO is underweight with high conviction, citing maturity extensions, PIK use and financial engineering in middle-market lending. AQR, at the 5.0 low end, models private credit as levered floating-rate HY with narrower spreads and lower cash rates. BNY expects relative performance to moderate.
Leans on: PIK and amendment activity, spread levels, the cash-rate path and a levered high-yield proxy.
What would settle it: PIK share, non-accruals and maturity extensions in direct lending portfolios, and realized default rates against public HY.

Method. Documents are collected monthly from the publishers' public pages. Each one is read into a fixed schema by a language model that must quote every number verbatim. A number that cannot be found in the document text is dropped. Ranges become midpoints. Real forecasts become nominal using the publisher's own inflation assumption. Arithmetic averages become geometric with the publisher's own volatility (g = a − σ²/2), so every figure compounds the same way. Proxies are tagged on hover (e.g. a Eurozone index in the Europe row, a total-market index in US large cap). Horizons and currencies are never adjusted: they are labelled, and non-USD figures stay out of the consensus. Only publishers whose material may be republished appear with numbers; the rest appear as their stated views, with a link to the original. Prediction-market odds are read-only public prices. Figures are the publishers' own estimates, not this site's, and nothing here is investment advice.

Third-party forecasts are attributed to their publishers and linked to the originals. Not investment advice.
