Summary

  • Equity medians (rows with n≥4): US large cap 6.0 (n=8) is lowest, then Japan 6.4, US small cap 6.5, global 6.8, EM 8.0, Europe 8.1; private equity tops all rows at 9.7 (n=5).
  • Fixed-income medians: cash 3.7 (n=6), intermediate Treasuries 4.9 (n=8), US aggregate 5.1 (n=6), IG 5.4 (n=6), high yield 5.9 (n=8); Treasury, IG and high-yield IQRs are only 23-27bp.
  • Widest disagreements, ranked by IQR among rows with n≥4: EM equity 250bp (n=7), commodities 171bp (n=7), US small cap 165bp (n=6), private equity 155bp (n=5); UK equity's 270bp rests on three publishers.
  • Ninety One sets the range low on US large cap (1.7), global equity (2.8) and EM equity (3.7), as expensive starting valuations drive negative revaluation in its model.
  • Among n≥4 rows, this site's LTCMA differs most on commodities (3.5 vs median 5.7, n=7) and US high yield (5.0 vs 5.9, n=8); it is also lower on cash, intermediate Treasuries and IG.
  • On n=3 rows this site's LTCMA is below for UK equity (7.0 vs 8.9) and China (7.4 vs 8.5), above for DM ex-US (7.7 vs 7.1); gold: 3.3 vs one publisher's 1.6.
  • Prediction markets price a 25bp hike at the next FOMC at 87.5-88.0% and zero 2026 cuts at 97.9-98.2%: supportive of Invesco's hike call, against Goldman Sachs AM's easing base case.
  • A US recession in 2026 is priced at 2.5% (Kalshi) and 4.2% (Polymarket), in line with expansion calls from Mercer, Robeco and UBS GWM CIO; PIMCO pairs resilient growth with a credit-loss cycle.

Material divergences

Rows where the publishers materially disagree: public IQR >= 150bp with n >= 4, or >= 1 overweight and >= 1 underweight stance. Arguments are paraphrased from the publishers' own documents.

US large cap — Median 6.0 (n=8), range 1.7-7.6; AI-driven earnings growth versus stretched starting valuations.

directional divergence · public IQR 92bp, n=8 · stances ▲2 ■1 ▼3
Higher-return / positive side: BNY Investments, Fidelity International, T. Rowe Price, AQR, Goldman Sachs AM
AI-lifted earnings can offset rich multiples. BNY holds valuations constant and gives the range high (7.6); Fidelity International lets AI-driven revenue growth outweigh above-equilibrium margins; AQR and T. Rowe Price (five-year horizon) land modestly above the median despite flagging rich valuations; Goldman Sachs AM cites megacap profit strength and sustained AI capex, favoring quality.
Leans on: Building blocks of income, inflation, real GDP and AI-boosted earnings or revenue growth; BNY assumes unchanged valuations; AQR adds expected inflation to a local real return.
Lower-return / negative side: Ninety One, State Street, Invesco, Vanguard, PIMCO, Lazard, Amundi
Ninety One's record-low estimate (1.7) reflects heavy negative revaluation; Invesco carries a sizeable valuation drag despite tech earnings and buybacks; Vanguard sees high valuations damping returns despite firmer earnings and margins. PIMCO puts the equity risk premium near post-war lows, favoring high-quality bonds; Lazard doubts US resilience despite AI; Amundi is underweight.
Leans on: Starting valuations and expected multiple change (State Street, Invesco, Ninety One revaluation); equity risk premium versus bond yields (PIMCO); US growth resilience (Lazard).
What would settle it: Whether AI-driven EPS growth outpaces multiple compression: forward earnings revisions and the equity risk premium versus Treasury yields.

US small cap — Median 6.5 (n=6), IQR 165bp; relative appeal over large caps versus weaker long-run earnings growth.

numeric divergence · public IQR 165bp, n=6 · stances ▲1 ■0 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: BNY Investments, AQR, Vanguard, Goldman Sachs AM
Vanguard and AQR place small caps above large caps; BNY's factor model keeps them close to broad US equity despite a weak size premium, with firms staying private for longer. Goldman Sachs AM points to attractive valuations, expected rate cuts, earnings acceleration and renewed M&A, while stressing active selection.
Leans on: Relative-return models placing small caps near or above large caps (Vanguard, AQR, BNY factor model); valuations, a Fed-cut path, earnings and M&A (Goldman Sachs AM).
Lower-return / negative side: State Street, Invesco, T. Rowe Price
State Street's building blocks give the range low (5.0); Invesco's estimate improved on a smaller valuation drag, but lower long-term earnings growth keeps small caps behind mid caps; T. Rowe Price's valuation-anchored, five-year forecast was selectively upgraded yet remains below the median.
Leans on: Dividend yield, real earnings growth and multiple change (State Street); long-term earnings growth and valuation drag (Invesco); starting valuations (T. Rowe Price).
What would settle it: Small-cap earnings delivery relative to large caps, and the Fed path: markets price zero 2026 cuts at 97.9-98.2%, undercutting Goldman Sachs AM's rate-cut leg.

EM equity — Widest IQR among rows with n≥4 (250bp); earnings momentum and weaker dollar versus revaluation risk.

numeric divergence · public IQR 250bp, n=7 · stances ▲6 ■0 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: Robeco, T. Rowe Price, BNY Investments, Goldman Sachs AM, BlackRock Investment Institute, Amundi, CIBC Asset Management
Robeco and T. Rowe Price (five-year horizons) give the top estimates on broader growth drivers and rebounding earnings; BNY sees the largest real-GDP lift to earnings plus currency gains. Goldman Sachs AM cites a softer dollar, Fed cuts and a wide P/E discount; BlackRock Investment Institute's overweight is highest-conviction; Amundi and CIBC Asset Management are overweight.
Leans on: Forward earnings momentum, real GDP growth feeding earnings, currency gains from a weaker dollar, and the P/E discount to US equities.
Lower-return / negative side: Ninety One, Vanguard, Invesco
Revaluation dominates for Ninety One, whose hedged estimate is the range low (3.7) and in the bottom quartile of its history; Vanguard lifted its estimate as valuations eased and fundamentals improved, yet stays below the median; Invesco ties results to earnings delivery and country valuations, with currency and dividends supportive.
Leans on: Starting valuations and revaluation, hedged to USD (Ninety One); MSCI EM valuations and fundamentals (Vanguard); earnings delivery, country valuations, currency and dividends (Invesco).
What would settle it: EM earnings delivery versus rebounded forward estimates, and the dollar; Goldman Sachs AM's Fed-cut leg faces a 25bp hike at the next FOMC priced at 87.5-88.0%.

US high yield — Tight numbers (IQR 27bp, median 5.9, n=8) but split stances on spreads and defaults.

directional divergence · public IQR 27bp, n=8 · stances ▲1 ■1 ▼2
Higher-return / positive side: Ninety One, BNY Investments, Goldman Sachs AM
Higher Treasury yields lifted Ninety One's estimate to the range high (6.3), though tight spreads and default losses keep it under the historic average; BNY nets defaults from yield and assumes spreads revert wider toward their long-run median. Goldman Sachs AM sees income backed by solid interest cover, low defaults, an upgraded ratings mix, steady demand and M&A.
Leans on: Starting Treasury yields plus spread, net of modeled default losses; BNY's spread-reversion assumption; Goldman Sachs AM's interest coverage, default, ratings-mix and demand data.
Lower-return / negative side: AQR, T. Rowe Price, State Street, PIMCO, Amundi
AQR's real return plus expected inflation gives the range low (5.0); T. Rowe Price (five-year horizon) allows for spread widening to erode part of the carry; State Street adjusts yields and spreads for default probability. PIMCO is wary of lower-quality, cyclical credit and reads tight spreads as complacency with a default cycle under way; Amundi is underweight.
Leans on: Real return plus expected inflation (AQR); default-adjusted yields and spreads (State Street); spread-widening risk to carry (T. Rowe Price); default-cycle and spread signals (PIMCO).
What would settle it: Realized HY default losses and whether spreads widen from near-historic tights, testing PIMCO's default-cycle call against Goldman Sachs AM's contained-defaults view.

Commodities — Range 2.3-6.7 (n=7, IQR 171bp); split on whether futures earn a durable premium over cash.

numeric divergence · public IQR 171bp, n=7 · stances ▲0 ■1 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: AQR, Invesco, Robeco
AQR assumes equal-weighted commodity futures earn a long-run excess return over cash, on top of a positive real cash return, giving the range high (6.7); Invesco values the asset's exposure to inflation and supply shocks as a distinct return source; Robeco (five-year horizon) sees outcomes driven by conflicts and the US dollar.
Leans on: Long-run futures excess return plus expected real cash yield (AQR); inflation and supply-shock sensitivity (Invesco); geopolitics and the dollar (Robeco).
Lower-return / negative side: BNY Investments, State Street, T. Rowe Price
BNY gives the range low (2.3): a low return with high volatility, little changed from its prior edition, with drivers unexplained; State Street builds from US inflation and dollar forecasts, futures roll, positioning and collateral return; T. Rowe Price, on a five-year horizon, gives no asset-specific rationale.
Leans on: US inflation and dollar-index forecasts, futures roll return, positioning and collateral yield (State Street); BNY and T. Rowe Price disclose no specific drivers.
What would settle it: Realized commodity excess returns over T-bills, including roll yield, against the dollar and inflation path; AQR's long-run futures premium is the swing assumption.

Global equity (ACWI) — Median 6.8 (n=6), range 2.8-7.8; earnings growth versus expensive valuations, T. Rowe Price at the median.

directional divergence · public IQR 100bp, n=6 · stances ▲1 ■0 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: Robeco, BNY Investments
Robeco (five-year horizon, developed-equity proxy) sees robust profit growth more than offsetting a de-rating, giving the range high (7.8); BNY, citing AI-driven productivity, aggregates regional income, earnings-growth and currency assumptions by market cap, consistent with the central path of its Monte Carlo simulation.
Leans on: Regional earnings growth, income and currency assumptions, cap-weighted (BNY); earnings growth net of expected multiple compression (Robeco, developed markets).
Lower-return / negative side: Ninety One, Invesco, Fidelity International, PIMCO
Ninety One's range low (2.8) reflects expensive valuations, notably in the US; PIMCO sees a better prospective Sharpe ratio in high-quality bonds, without calling a correction; Invesco's softer earnings growth and lower buybacks partly offset a smaller valuation drag. Fidelity International sits just below the median yet stays overweight, weighing AI-driven GDP gains against above-equilibrium margins.
Leans on: Starting valuations and revaluation (Ninety One); bond-versus-equity Sharpe ratios (PIMCO); earnings growth, buybacks and valuation drag (Invesco); margins versus equilibrium (Fidelity, MSCI World).
What would settle it: Global forward P/E and earnings revisions: a de-rating without EPS growth supports Ninety One and PIMCO; earnings outpacing multiple compression supports Robeco and BNY.

Global govt bonds — Global government bonds: numbers tight (IQR 39bp, n=4) but stances split on fiscal strain versus diversification value.

directional divergence · public IQR 39bp, n=4 · stances ▲2 ■1 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: Robeco, Ninety One, PIMCO
Robeco (USD-hedged, five-year horizon) credits higher yields for better expected returns, with caveats; Ninety One puts hedged developed sovereign returns near the middle of their history. PIMCO's top-conviction view: out-of-sync cycles and diverging central-bank paths make global sovereigns a diversifier and income source with room for active country and curve positioning.
Leans on: Starting yields hedged to USD versus their history (Robeco, Ninety One); cross-country policy divergence and cycle desynchronization (PIMCO).
Lower-return / negative side: Invesco, Fidelity International, BNY Investments
Invesco builds its below-median figure on income from current yields; Fidelity International, at the range low (4.8), is nonetheless overweight, expecting sovereigns to diversify in recessionary shocks. BNY sees fiscal strain eroding sovereigns' risk-adjusted appeal and looks to real assets and private markets to diversify.
Leans on: Current yields as the income driver (Invesco, Fidelity); diversification in recessionary shocks (Fidelity); fiscal strain and steeper curves (BNY).
What would settle it: Term premia and curve steepening under fiscal strain, and bond-equity correlation in sell-offs; markets price a US recession in 2026 at 2.5% (Kalshi) and 4.2% (Polymarket).

Private equity — Highest median (9.7, n=5), equal to Fidelity International's estimate; range 6.6-10.5 hinges on the illiquidity premium.

numeric divergence · public IQR 155bp, n=5 · stances ▲4 ■0 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: BNY Investments, T. Rowe Price, BlackRock Investment Institute, CIBC Asset Management
BNY adds a strong illiquidity premium to public-equity factor exposure, as private capital funds AI and infrastructure, for the range high (10.5); T. Rowe Price (five-year horizon) sees the illiquidity premium and active management keeping PE ahead of public equity. BlackRock Investment Institute flags venture capital as a high-conviction alpha opportunity; CIBC Asset Management is overweight.
Leans on: Public-equity factor returns plus an illiquidity premium (BNY); Cambridge Associates global PE benchmark with illiquidity and active-management premia (T. Rowe Price).
Lower-return / negative side: AQR, State Street
AQR values US buyouts with a levered dividend discount model net of fees and sees compressed risk premia in private assets; lower debt costs and multiple expansion lifted its estimate, which remains the range low (6.6). State Street extrapolates historical fund performance, close to levered listed-equity returns once fees are deducted.
Leans on: Levered DDM on US buyouts with fee, debt-cost and multiple assumptions (AQR); extrapolated historical fund returns versus leverage-adjusted listed equity (State Street).
What would settle it: Realized net-of-fee PE returns versus leverage-adjusted listed equity, as higher rates and elevated dry powder test whether an illiquidity premium survives fees.

Private credit — Range 5.0-9.2 (n=4); PIMCO flags rising direct-lending stress, every other stated stance is overweight.

directional divergence · public IQR 127bp, n=4 · stances ▲6 ■0 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: T. Rowe Price, State Street, Goldman Sachs AM, BlackRock Investment Institute, Amundi, CIBC Asset Management
T. Rowe Price (five-year horizon) gives the range high (9.2) on the illiquidity premium and lasting demand for private financing; State Street's method is undisclosed. Goldman Sachs AM cites higher yields than public credit and a better default record than syndicated loans; BlackRock Investment Institute backs selected private credit; Amundi and CIBC Asset Management are overweight.
Leans on: Illiquidity premium and structural demand (T. Rowe Price, Cliffwater direct-lending index); yield over public credit and default history versus syndicated loans (Goldman Sachs AM).
Lower-return / negative side: AQR, Fidelity International, PIMCO
AQR models private credit as levered floating-rate high yield, with fees cancelling any illiquidity premium; narrower spreads and lower cash rates cut its estimate, now the range low (5.0). PIMCO expects markedly higher losses as middle-market lending shows more PIK, extended maturities and mounting stress. Fidelity International, below the median, stays overweight on higher-rate income.
Leans on: Levered floating-rate HY net of fees, spreads and cash rates (AQR); PIK, maturity-extension and stress trends in middle-market lending, excluding asset-based finance (PIMCO).
What would settle it: Direct-lending non-accruals, PIK share and loss rates; a 25bp hike at the next FOMC, priced at 87.5-88.0%, lifts floating-rate income but adds refinancing pressure.

Method. The publishers' public pages are checked every weekday; when a document has a new edition it is read again and the consensus is rebuilt. Each one is read into a fixed schema by a language model that must quote every number verbatim. A number that cannot be found in the document text is dropped. Ranges become midpoints. Real forecasts become nominal using the publisher's own inflation assumption. Arithmetic averages become geometric with the publisher's own volatility (g = a − σ²/2), so every figure compounds the same way. Proxies are tagged on hover (e.g. a Eurozone index in the Europe row, a total-market index in US large cap). Horizons and currencies are never adjusted: they are labelled, and non-USD figures stay out of the consensus. Only publishers whose material may be republished appear with numbers; the rest appear as their stated views, with a link to the original. Prediction-market odds are read-only public prices. Figures are the publishers' own estimates, not this site's, and nothing here is investment advice.

Third-party forecasts are attributed to their publishers and linked to the originals. Not investment advice.
