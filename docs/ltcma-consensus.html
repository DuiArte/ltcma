  • US recession in 2026 priced at 3.5% (Kalshi) and 6.5% (Polymarket; contract terms differ), siding with the resilient-growth calls of Vanguard, Invesco and Mercer over Lazard's doubts on US resilience.

Material divergences

Rows where the publishers materially disagree: public IQR >= 150bp with n >= 4, or >= 1 overweight and >= 1 underweight stance. Arguments are paraphrased from the publishers' own documents.

US large cap — AI-driven earnings optimism versus valuation drag; seven estimates span 4.9-7.6 around a 6.3 median.

directional divergence · public IQR 110bp, n=7 · stances ▲2 ■0 ▼3
Higher-return / positive side: BNY Investments, T. Rowe Price, Fidelity International, Goldman Sachs AM, BlackRock Investment Institute
BNY Investments (7.6) assumes AI lifts earnings with valuations held flat; Fidelity International (6.4) counts on AI-aided revenue growth and payouts, net of a margin drag. Goldman Sachs AM and BlackRock Investment Institute, overweight, cite AI-driven earnings strength. T. Rowe Price (6.4) sits barely above the median, still citing rich valuations.
Leans on: Income, inflation and real-growth building blocks with an AI uplift to earnings; no valuation mean-reversion (BNY Investments); revenue growth, dividends, buybacks and margin normalization (Fidelity International).
Lower-return / negative side: State Street, Vanguard, Invesco, PIMCO, Lazard, Amundi
Invesco (4.9) lets a steep multiple-compression drag offset real earnings growth and shareholder yield; Vanguard (5.1) says higher valuations cut returns; State Street (5.5) tempers its building blocks for valuation. PIMCO, underweight, sees almost no equity risk premium and a better bond Sharpe ratio; Lazard expects rotation away from US equities; Amundi is underweight.
Leans on: Starting multiples with assumed valuation mean-reversion, layered on dividend or shareholder yield, real earnings growth and inflation; equity risk premium versus bond yields (PIMCO).
What would settle it: Whether AI-linked mega-cap earnings growth and margins hold up enough to sustain current multiples; P/E compression without matching earnings gains would support the lower-return camp.

US small cap — Second-widest IQR of any row (188bp); valuation-anchored optimists versus rich-after-rally and weak-earnings views.

numeric divergence · public IQR 188bp, n=6 · stances ▲1 ■0 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: AQR, BNY Investments, T. Rowe Price, Goldman Sachs AM
AQR (7.4) adds expected inflation to a valuation-based real return; BNY Investments (7.5) assumes a weak size premium as firms stay private longer, yet lands just under its broad-equity figure. T. Rowe Price (6.3, five-year horizon) upgraded the segment. Goldman Sachs AM, overweight, cites attractive valuations, expected Fed cuts, accelerating earnings and revived dealmaking.
Leans on: Valuation-based real return plus inflation (AQR); factor exposures and size premium (BNY Investments); valuation-anchored index forecast (T. Rowe Price); expected cuts, earnings momentum, M&A (Goldman Sachs AM).
Lower-return / negative side: State Street, Vanguard, Invesco
Invesco (4.5, bottom of range) sees a smaller valuation drag than in large caps but weak long-run earnings growth, ranking small caps behind mid-caps; Vanguard (5.7) says the strong small-cap rally lifted valuations and cut expected returns much as for large caps; State Street (5.1) assumes higher volatility than for large caps.
Leans on: Starting valuations after the rally (Vanguard); long-run earnings growth and valuation drag in a building-block model (Invesco); volatility assumptions (State Street).
What would settle it: Small-cap earnings versus Invesco's weak-growth assumption, and the Fed: Goldman Sachs AM leans on cuts; markets put zero cuts in 2026 at 95.7% (Kalshi) and 95.8% (Polymarket).

US high yield — Carry and contained defaults versus near-record-tight spreads and an emerging default cycle.

directional divergence · public IQR 90bp, n=7 · stances ▲1 ■0 ▼2
Higher-return / positive side: Invesco, BNY Investments, Fidelity International, Goldman Sachs AM
Starting yield dominates: Invesco (6.1) nets credit losses and spread drag off a high average yield; BNY Investments (6.1) matches it while assuming spreads widen toward their long-run median; Fidelity International (5.9) keeps a modest premium over IG. Goldman Sachs AM, overweight, cites strong interest coverage, contained defaults, a better ratings mix and firm demand.
Leans on: Average yield minus expected credit losses; spread mean-reversion with defaults tied to spread (BNY Investments); interest coverage, ratings mix, defaults, M&A demand (Goldman Sachs AM).
Lower-return / negative side: State Street, Vanguard, AQR, PIMCO, Amundi
Compensation is thin: Vanguard (5.2) calls credit valuations rich; State Street (4.6, bottom of range) finds only a modest premium over IG from spread term structure and default probabilities; AQR (5.0) sits below the median. PIMCO, underweight, reads near-record-tight spreads as complacent while defaults climb, and leans up in quality; Amundi is also underweight.
Leans on: Spread term structure and default probabilities (State Street); credit valuations (Vanguard); spreads versus historic tights and rising defaults (PIMCO).
What would settle it: High-yield default rates and spreads: rising defaults with widening spreads would confirm PIMCO's default-cycle call; contained defaults let starting carry dominate, as the higher camp assumes.

Commodities — Widest IQR of any row (225bp); AQR's futures premium versus State Street's roll-plus-collateral build; rationale often missing.

numeric divergence · public IQR 225bp, n=7 · stances ▲0 ■1 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: Vanguard, AQR, Robeco
AQR (6.7, top of range) assumes equal-weighted futures keep earning a long-run excess return over cash, on top of a positive real cash return. Vanguard (5.7) reports a model output for a broad commodity index without commodity-specific commentary. Robeco (5.8, five-year horizon) ties outcomes to geopolitical conflicts and the dollar path.
Leans on: Long-run futures excess return over cash plus real cash yield (AQR); index-proxy model output (Vanguard); conflict and US dollar scenarios (Robeco).
Lower-return / negative side: State Street, Invesco, BNY Investments
State Street (3.2) builds returns from US inflation and dollar forecasts, a long-run roll return and collateral yield; Invesco (3.8) edged up on a firmer real-asset outlook and stresses diversifying return drivers; BNY Investments (2.3, bottom of range) expects low returns with high volatility, little changed, without explaining the drivers.
Leans on: Inflation, dollar, roll-return and collateral building blocks (State Street); real-asset outlook (Invesco); BNY Investments discloses no drivers.
What would settle it: Realized roll yield and collateral returns versus the long-run futures premium AQR assumes, plus the dollar path that both State Street and Robeco flag.

Global govt bonds — Global government bonds: PIMCO's high-conviction overweight versus BNY Investments' fiscal-strain underweight; estimates tightly clustered (IQR 23bp, n=3).

directional divergence · public IQR 23bp, n=3 · stances ▲2 ■1 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: Robeco, PIMCO
Higher starting yields have lifted expected returns: Robeco (5.3, top of range; USD-hedged, five-year horizon) makes that case while cautioning the gain is not costless. PIMCO, overweight with high conviction, sees desynchronized cycles and diverging central-bank paths creating country and curve opportunities, with global sovereigns adding income and diversification.
Leans on: Current sovereign yields hedged to USD (Robeco); cross-country divergence in business cycles and monetary policy (PIMCO).
Lower-return / negative side: Fidelity International, BNY Investments
BNY Investments, underweight, argues fiscal strain makes sovereign debt less attractive on a risk-adjusted basis and looks to real assets and private markets for diversification. Fidelity International sits here only on its number, just below the median (4.8 vs 4.9); its stance is overweight, citing income from higher curves and diversification in recessionary shocks.
Leans on: Fiscal strain and expected curve steepening (BNY Investments); current government yield curves as the income driver (Fidelity International).
What would settle it: How sovereigns trade in the next growth scare: falling yields would support the diversification case; rising term premia on fiscal news would support BNY Investments' caution.

Private equity — Illiquidity-premium optimists versus leverage-adjusted, net-of-fee models; estimates span 6.6-10.5 around a 9.7 median.

numeric divergence · public IQR 155bp, n=5 · stances ▲2 ■0 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: BNY Investments, T. Rowe Price
BNY Investments (10.5, top of range), overweight, layers a large illiquidity premium on public-equity factor exposure and expects private capital to finance AI innovation and infrastructure. T. Rowe Price, on a five-year horizon, makes private equity its highest forecast, citing the illiquidity premium and active management relative to public equity.
Leans on: Public-equity factor exposures plus an illiquidity premium (BNY Investments); Cambridge Associates global PE benchmark (T. Rowe Price).
Lower-return / negative side: State Street, AQR
AQR (6.6, bottom of range) models US buyouts as levered equity in a dividend discount model, net of fees; cheaper debt and more assumed multiple expansion lifted its estimate, yet it sees compressed private-asset premia versus positive real cash. State Street benchmarks historical private-equity returns to listed equities, adjusting for leverage and fees.
Leans on: Levered dividend-discount model with fee drag, debt cost and multiple assumptions (AQR); historical PE-versus-listed performance, leverage-adjusted and net of fees (State Street).
What would settle it: Whether net-of-fee buyout returns keep a premium over listed equity after leverage adjustment, and the debt costs and exit multiples that drive AQR's model.

Private credit — Income and illiquidity-premium case versus PIMCO's stress warnings and AQR's fee arithmetic; estimates span 5.0-9.2 (n=4).

directional divergence · public IQR 143bp, n=4 · stances ▲4 ■0 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: T. Rowe Price, Fidelity International, Goldman Sachs AM, BlackRock Investment Institute, Amundi
T. Rowe Price (9.2, five-year horizon) cites an illiquidity premium and structural demand for private capital; Fidelity International (6.9) sees higher-rate income and no immediate stress. Goldman Sachs AM (higher yields than public credit, historically fewer defaults than syndicated loans), BlackRock Investment Institute (selected deals with lender protections) and Amundi are overweight.
Leans on: Cliffwater Direct Lending index (T. Rowe Price); floating-rate income at elevated policy rates; historical default rates versus syndicated loans; underwriting and lender protections.
Lower-return / negative side: State Street, AQR, PIMCO
AQR (5.0, bottom of range) models direct lending as levered floating-rate high yield whose fees absorb any illiquidity premium. State Street (6.4), just below the median, adds a senior-lending premium to public credit, net of fees. PIMCO, a high-conviction underweight outside asset-based finance, sees stress in middle-market direct lending and risk of poor recoveries.
Leans on: Public high-yield spreads, cash rates and fee drag in a levered model (AQR); senior-lending premium over public credit (State Street); PIK use, maturity extensions and recoveries (PIMCO).
What would settle it: Middle-market default, PIK and recovery data, plus the cash-rate path behind floating-rate income: rising stress would back PIMCO; benign credit with high rates, the income camp.

Method. Documents are collected monthly from the publishers' public pages. Each one is read into a fixed schema by a language model that must quote every number verbatim. A number that cannot be found in the document text is dropped. Ranges become midpoints. Real forecasts become nominal using the publisher's own inflation assumption. Arithmetic averages become geometric with the publisher's own volatility (g = a − σ²/2), so every figure compounds the same way. Proxies are tagged on hover (e.g. a Eurozone index in the Europe row, a total-market index in US large cap). Horizons and currencies are never adjusted: they are labelled, and non-USD figures stay out of the consensus. Only publishers whose material may be republished appear with numbers; the rest appear as their stated views, with a link to the original. Prediction-market odds are read-only public prices. Figures are the publishers' own estimates, not this site's, and nothing here is investment advice.

Third-party forecasts are attributed to their publishers and linked to the originals. Not investment advice.
