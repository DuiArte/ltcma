Material divergences

Rows where the publishers materially disagree: public IQR >= 150bp with n >= 4, or >= 1 overweight and >= 1 underweight stance. Arguments are paraphrased from the publishers' own documents.

US large cap — Median 6.0 (n=8); range 1.7-7.6 hinges on whether multiples revert or AI earnings carry.

directional divergence · public IQR 92bp, n=8 · stances ▲2 ■1 ▼3
Higher-return / positive side: BNY Investments, T. Rowe Price, Fidelity International, AQR, Goldman Sachs AM
BNY Investments (7.6) keeps valuations unchanged and adds AI-aided profit-share gains to real GDP, inflation and income; Fidelity International (6.4) adds AI revenue growth, dividends and repurchases despite elevated margins; T. Rowe Price (6.4, 5y) trims its prior figure; AQR (6.3) adds expected inflation to its real return. Goldman Sachs AM cites mega-cap earnings strength and sustained AI investment.
Leans on: Building blocks of income, inflation, real GDP and AI-linked earnings or revenue growth plus repurchases; BNY assumes no multiple change; AQR: valuation-based real return plus inflation.
Lower-return / negative side: Ninety One, State Street, Invesco, Vanguard, PIMCO, Lazard, Amundi
Valuations dominate. Ninety One (1.7), its lowest forecast on record, assumes the harshest de-rating, rivalled only near the dot-com peak; Invesco (5.5) keeps a material valuation drag despite AI and repurchases; Vanguard (5.7): high valuations temper earnings gains; State Street (5.4) nets in multiple change. PIMCO sees a negligible equity risk premium; Lazard doubts US resilience; Amundi is underweight.
Leans on: Starting valuations and mean-reverting multiples (Ninety One, Invesco, Vanguard), building blocks with multiple change (State Street), equity risk premium versus bond yields (PIMCO).
What would settle it: Forward P/E versus AI-driven earnings delivery: multiple compression without an earnings offset supports the lower side; sustained mega-cap earnings beats at stable multiples support the higher side.

US small cap — IQR 165bp (n=6) hinges on whether small caps earn a premium over large caps.

numeric divergence · public IQR 165bp, n=6 · stances ▲1 ■0 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: BNY Investments, AQR, Vanguard, Goldman Sachs AM
Vanguard (6.6) and AQR (7.4) put small caps above their own large-cap figures; BNY Investments (7.5) sits just under its broad-US figure, trimming for a weak size premium as companies list later. Goldman Sachs AM cites cheap valuations, anticipated Fed easing, faster profit growth and a pickup in deals.
Leans on: AQR: small-cap real return plus US inflation; BNY: factor model with a weak size premium; Goldman Sachs AM: valuations, a rate-cut path, earnings acceleration and M&A activity.
Lower-return / negative side: State Street, Invesco, T. Rowe Price
No size premium in these models: State Street (5.0), Invesco (5.3) and T. Rowe Price (6.3, 5y) all sit below their own large-cap figures. Invesco's figure improved as valuation drag shrank, but weaker long-run earnings growth leaves small caps behind mid caps; T. Rowe's selective upgrade stays valuation-anchored.
Leans on: Dividend yield, real earnings growth and multiple change (State Street); valuation drag and long-run earnings growth (Invesco); starting valuations (T. Rowe Price).
What would settle it: Small-versus-large earnings growth and the Fed path: Goldman Sachs AM's case assumes cuts, while zero 2026 cuts are priced at 97.6% (Kalshi) and 97.9% (Polymarket).

EM equity — Widest IQR among n≥4 rows, 250bp (n=7); hedged Ninety One 3.7 versus Robeco 8.8.

numeric divergence · public IQR 250bp, n=7 · stances ▲7 ■0 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: Robeco, T. Rowe Price, BNY Investments, Goldman Sachs AM, UBS GWM CIO, BlackRock Investment Institute, Amundi, CIBC Asset Management
BNY Investments (8.1) credits the largest real-GDP lift to earnings plus mild weaker-dollar currency gains; T. Rowe Price (8.7, 5y) cites rebounding forward earnings; Robeco (8.8, 5y) ranks EM its top asset for USD investors. Goldman Sachs AM, UBS GWM CIO and BlackRock Investment Institute cite earnings, valuation discounts and liquidity; Amundi and CIBC are overweight.
Leans on: Real GDP growth feeding earnings, forward-earnings revisions, P/E discount to the US, weaker-dollar currency gains, retail inflows; Goldman Sachs AM also assumes Fed cuts and lower oil.
Lower-return / negative side: Ninety One, Vanguard, Invesco
Ninety One (3.7) is USD-hedged: carry is added to the local forecast, yet the result ranks in the bottom quartile of historical decade outcomes. Vanguard (4.7, unhedged) raised its view on easier valuations and firmer fundamentals but notes the highest equity volatility; Invesco (7.1) sees currency and dividend support, hinging on whether earnings materialise and on country-level valuations.
Leans on: Hedged local forecast plus carry versus the historical return distribution (Ninety One); valuations and fundamentals on unhedged MSCI EM (Vanguard); realised earnings and country valuation levels (Invesco).
What would settle it: EM earnings delivery against rebounded forward estimates, the dollar's path, and whether the P/E discount to the US narrows.

US high yield — Numbers cluster (IQR 27bp, n=8) around a 5.9 median, but stances split on spreads and defaults.

directional divergence · public IQR 27bp, n=8 · stances ▲1 ■1 ▼2
Higher-return / positive side: Ninety One, BNY Investments, Goldman Sachs AM
Ninety One (6.3) raised its forecast as risk-free yields rose, while narrow spreads and default losses hold it under its historical average; BNY Investments subtracts defaults from yield and assumes spreads revert toward their long-run median. Goldman Sachs AM cites healthy interest cover, low defaults, higher average ratings, steady demand and deal activity.
Leans on: Starting yields lifted by higher risk-free rates; default losses (Ninety One, BNY) and BNY's spread reversion; credit fundamentals and demand (Goldman Sachs AM).
Lower-return / negative side: AQR, T. Rowe Price, State Street, PIMCO, Amundi
Tight spreads leave little cushion: AQR (5.0) builds from real return plus US inflation; T. Rowe Price (5.5, 5y) expects possible spread widening to absorb part of the higher carry; State Street (5.7) haircuts yields and spreads for default probability. PIMCO is wary of lower-quality, cyclical credit, reading tight spreads as complacency as defaults rise; Amundi is underweight.
Leans on: Spreads near historical tights, default-probability adjustments (State Street), real return plus inflation (AQR), rising stress in lower-quality credit (PIMCO).
What would settle it: Default rates and spread moves from current tights; recession odds are low at 2.5% (Kalshi) and 4.5% (Polymarket), so the test is a credit-specific default cycle.

Commodities — IQR 171bp (n=7); split turns on whether futures earn a durable excess return over cash.

numeric divergence · public IQR 171bp, n=7 · stances ▲1 ■1 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: AQR, Invesco, Robeco, UBS GWM CIO
AQR (6.7) assumes equal-weighted futures keep a long-run excess return over cash, added to a positive expected real cash return; Invesco (6.5) leans on sensitivity to inflation surprises and supply disruptions; Robeco (5.8, 5y) links results to geopolitical conflict and the dollar. UBS GWM CIO sees constrained supply meeting stronger demand in energy, metals and farm goods.
Leans on: Long-run futures excess return plus expected real cash (AQR), inflation and supply-shock sensitivity (Invesco), geopolitics and the dollar (Robeco), supply-demand balances (UBS GWM CIO).
Lower-return / negative side: BNY Investments, State Street, T. Rowe Price
BNY Investments (2.3) pairs a low return with high volatility, barely revised, and gives no drivers; State Street (3.6) combines expected US inflation, a dollar view, roll yield, positioning and collateral income; T. Rowe Price (5.2, 5y) offers no commodity-specific rationale.
Leans on: Inflation and dollar projections, roll yield, positioning and collateral income (State Street); BNY and T. Rowe Price disclose no specific drivers.
What would settle it: Realised roll and excess returns of commodity futures over T-bills, and the dollar's trend, which State Street and Robeco both tie to returns.

Global equity (ACWI) — Median 6.8 (n=6), where T. Rowe Price sits; Ninety One's hedged 2.8 is the outlier.

directional divergence · public IQR 100bp, n=6 · stances ▲2 ■0 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: Robeco, BNY Investments, UBS GWM CIO
Earnings growth outruns de-rating: Robeco (7.8, 5y, developed-market proxy) expects strong profit growth to beat multiple compression; BNY Investments cap-weights regional building blocks (yield, profit growth, FX), consistent with the median of its Monte Carlo simulations. UBS GWM CIO sees a widening rally powered by faster growth, monetary easing, fiscal support and AI spending.
Leans on: Regional income, earnings and currency estimates, cap-weighted (BNY); earnings growth versus multiple compression on developed equities (Robeco); growth, policy easing and AI capex (UBS GWM CIO).
Lower-return / negative side: Ninety One, Invesco, Fidelity International, PIMCO
Ninety One (2.8) adds modest hedging carry to a weak local-currency forecast driven by rich US valuations; Invesco (6.4) rose as valuation drag eased and FX added, while weaker profit growth and fewer repurchases pared the gain; Fidelity International (6.7, MSCI World), though overweight, nets above-equilibrium margins against AI-driven growth. PIMCO rates quality bonds' expected Sharpe ratio above equities'.
Leans on: Starting valuations, US weight and hedging carry (Ninety One); valuation drag, earnings growth and repurchases (Invesco); margin mean-reversion (Fidelity); bond-versus-equity Sharpe ratios (PIMCO).
What would settle it: Whether US multiples compress faster than earnings grow, and the dollar's path, which drives the currency contribution in BNY's and Invesco's figures.

Global govt bonds — Tight numbers (IQR 39bp, n=4); PIMCO overweight on diversification, BNY underweight on fiscal strain.

directional divergence · public IQR 39bp, n=4 · stances ▲2 ■1 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: Robeco, Ninety One, PIMCO
Robeco (5.3, 5y) credits higher yields for better USD-hedged sovereign returns, while cautioning they carry risks; Ninety One (5.3) sees developed yields close to their highs of the inflation-targeting era, putting returns mid-distribution historically. PIMCO rates global sovereigns a leading conviction: they diversify, pay income and reward country and curve selection as cycles and policies diverge.
Leans on: USD-hedged developed sovereign composites (Robeco, Ninety One) starting from higher yields; cross-country policy divergence and curve opportunities (PIMCO).
Lower-return / negative side: Invesco, Fidelity International, BNY Investments
Returns rest on starting income with little else: Invesco (4.9) anchors on current government yields; Fidelity International (4.8), despite an overweight, treats income as the main driver and values sovereigns as recession hedges. BNY Investments sees fiscal strain eroding sovereigns' risk-adjusted appeal and prefers real and private assets as diversifiers.
Leans on: Current government yield levels as the income anchor (Invesco, Fidelity); sovereign fiscal strain and steeper curves (BNY Investments).
What would settle it: Term premia and curve steepness under fiscal strain, whether policy paths keep diverging across regions, and rate differentials that set USD-hedging carry.

Private equity — Highest median, 9.7 (n=5), where Fidelity International sits; illiquidity premium versus levered public beta after fees.

numeric divergence · public IQR 155bp, n=5 · stances ▲3 ■0 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: BNY Investments, T. Rowe Price, CIBC Asset Management
BNY Investments (10.5) adds a sizeable illiquidity premium to listed-equity factor betas, citing private funding of AI and infrastructure; T. Rowe Price (Cambridge Associates Global PE, 5y) credits illiquidity compensation and manager skill for an edge over listed equity. CIBC Asset Management is overweight.
Leans on: Listed-equity factor betas plus an assumed illiquidity premium (BNY); Cambridge Associates Global PE benchmark with illiquidity and active-management premia (T. Rowe Price).
Lower-return / negative side: AQR, State Street
AQR (6.6) values US LBOs as levered equity through a dividend discount model and deducts fees, so compressed equity premia pass straight through; cheaper debt and multiple expansion lifted it. State Street projects historical fund performance forward, close to what levered listed equity delivers net of fees. Both leave little net premium over levered public beta.
Leans on: Levered dividend-discount model with fees, debt cost and multiple assumptions (AQR); historical fund returns versus leverage-adjusted listed equity (State Street).
What would settle it: Realised net-of-fee PE returns versus leverage-adjusted listed equity, exit and distribution activity, and LBO financing costs as rate expectations rise.

Private credit — IQR 127bp (n=4); fee-adjusted spread math versus illiquidity premium, with PIMCO flagging a loss cycle.

directional divergence · public IQR 127bp, n=4 · stances ▲6 ■0 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: T. Rowe Price, State Street, Goldman Sachs AM, BlackRock Investment Institute, Amundi, CIBC Asset Management
T. Rowe Price (9.2, 5y, direct lending) credits illiquidity compensation and lasting appetite for private financing; State Street (7.2) tracks senior private debt, net of fees. Goldman Sachs AM sees extra yield versus public credit and a better default record than syndicated loans; BlackRock Investment Institute is overweight only selectively, as an alpha source; Amundi and CIBC are overweight.

Method. Documents are collected monthly from the publishers' public pages. Each one is read into a fixed schema by a language model that must quote every number verbatim. A number that cannot be found in the document text is dropped. Ranges become midpoints. Real forecasts become nominal using the publisher's own inflation assumption. Arithmetic averages become geometric with the publisher's own volatility (g = a − σ²/2), so every figure compounds the same way. Proxies are tagged on hover (e.g. a Eurozone index in the Europe row, a total-market index in US large cap). Horizons and currencies are never adjusted: they are labelled, and non-USD figures stay out of the consensus. Only publishers whose material may be republished appear with numbers; the rest appear as their stated views, with a link to the original. Prediction-market odds are read-only public prices. Figures are the publishers' own estimates, not this site's, and nothing here is investment advice.

Third-party forecasts are attributed to their publishers and linked to the originals. Not investment advice.
