Summary

  • Equities: US large cap median 6.3% (n=7) trails global equity at 6.8% (n=5) and EM equity at 8.1% (n=6); US small cap sits at 6.0% (n=6). Private equity has the highest median, 9.7% (n=5).
  • Bonds: cash 3.6% (n=6), intermediate Treasuries 4.8% (n=7), IG corporate 5.2% (n=5), high yield 5.5% (n=7), EM USD sovereign 5.7% (n=8); credit adds little over Treasuries, matching tight-spread commentary.
  • Widest disagreements, ranked by IQR (rows with n≥4): commodities 225bp, US small cap 188bp, private equity 155bp, private credit 143bp, EM equity 122bp; tightest are US TIPS 37bp and EM local debt 41bp.
  • Stated stances lean underweight US large cap (six to one) and overweight EM (seven to one) and Japan equity (five to one); PIMCO's underweights in high yield, loans and private credit oppose GSAM's overweights.
  • This site's LTCMA sits furthest from a well-populated median on commodities: 3.5% vs 5.2% (170bp below, n=7). UK equity (7.0% vs 9.0%, two publishers) and gold (3.3% vs 1.6%, one publisher) are thin comparisons.
  • This site's LTCMA is above the public median on US TIPS (4.5% vs 3.8%, n=4), below on US high yield (5.0% vs 5.5%, n=7) and US large cap (6.0% vs 6.3%, n=7).
  • Next FOMC: a hold priced at 83.5% on both venues, a 25bp hike at 15.5-16.0%; zero 2026 cuts near-certain (95.8-95.9%), at odds with GSAM, UBS and BNY easing calls; Vanguard's higher-for-longer fits.
  • US recession in 2026 is priced at 3.5% (Kalshi) and 6.5% (Polymarket), consistent with GSAM's soft landing; Polymarket gives Democrats the House at 92.5% and Senate at 64.5%, relevant to fiscal-support calls.

Material divergences

Rows where the publishers materially disagree: public IQR >= 150bp with n >= 4, or >= 1 overweight and >= 1 underweight stance. Arguments are paraphrased from the publishers' own documents.

US large cap — Stances lean underweight six to one; public numbers split 4.9-5.5% versus 6.4-7.6% around a 6.3% median.

directional divergence · public IQR 110bp, n=7 · stances ▲1 ■4 ▼6
Higher-return / positive side: BNY Investments, Fidelity International, T. Rowe Price, BlackRock Investment Institute
BNY's Grinold-Kroner build, the range high at 7.6%, pairs buyback-heavy income with AI-boosted earnings growth at constant valuations. Fidelity's 6.4% weighs AI-boosted GDP growth against elevated margins and reverting valuations. BII's high-conviction overweight cites AI-driven earnings and supportive macro. T. Rowe Price's 6.4% (five-year) sits just above the median despite an underweight stance.
Leans on: Net payout yield (dividends plus buybacks), AI-driven earnings and GDP growth, constant or only partly reverting valuation multiples.
Lower-return / negative side: Invesco, Vanguard, State Street, PIMCO, Lazard
Invesco's 4.9%, the range low, nets a large valuation mean-reversion drag against strong earnings and buybacks; Vanguard's 5.1% reflects valuations rising from already elevated levels; State Street's 5.5% tempers its building blocks for valuation. PIMCO sees a near-zero equity risk premium, with high-quality bonds offering a comparable Sharpe ratio; Lazard expects allocators to rotate away from US equities.
Leans on: Starting valuations with mean reversion, equity risk premium versus high-quality bond yields, dividend yield plus real earnings growth plus inflation.
What would settle it: Whether forward multiples compress while AI-led earnings growth holds, and the earnings-yield gap over Treasuries; the split is mostly a valuation-path assumption.

US small cap — IQR of 188bp, second only to commodities; numbers 4.5-7.5% split on earnings growth and size premium.

both divergence · public IQR 188bp, n=6 · stances ▲1 ■0 ▼2
Higher-return / positive side: BNY Investments, AQR, T. Rowe Price, Goldman Sachs AM
GSAM is overweight on attractive valuations, anticipated rate cuts, accelerating earnings and a dealmaking revival. AQR's 7.4% and T. Rowe Price's 6.3% (five-year, a selective upgrade) also sit above the median. BNY has the range high, 7.5%, yet is underweight, citing a muted size premium as early-stage growth shifts to private markets.
Leans on: Relative valuation versus large caps, expected rate cuts, earnings acceleration and M&A (GSAM); real return plus inflation (AQR); factor-model size premium (BNY).
Lower-return / negative side: Invesco, Vanguard, State Street
Invesco (underweight, 4.5%, the range low) sees less valuation drag than large caps but weak long-run earnings growth. Vanguard's 5.7% reflects a second-quarter rally that raised valuations, cutting its forecast much as for large caps. State Street's 5.1% sits below its own large-cap 5.5%, with higher volatility. All three are below the 6.0% median.
Leans on: Post-rally starting valuations, long-run earnings growth of smaller firms, higher expected volatility.
What would settle it: Small-cap earnings growth versus large caps, relative valuation after the rally, and policy rates: zero 2026 Fed cuts are priced at 95.8-95.9% across Kalshi and Polymarket.

China equity — Only two public numbers, 5.8% and 8.7%; overweights cite valuations, Lazard sees fragility needing intervention.

directional divergence · public IQR 145bp, n=2 · stances ▲2 ■1 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: T. Rowe Price, Goldman Sachs AM, Amundi
GSAM is overweight on attractive valuations and light positioning, while warning that durability depends on earnings and flagging tariff and policy shocks. Amundi is overweight because lagging performance left valuations attractive. T. Rowe Price's 8.7% (five-year) pairs a high return with its highest equity volatility, helped by an EM earnings rebound.
Leans on: Starting valuations and investor positioning (GSAM, Amundi); EM earnings rebound and a high volatility assumption (T. Rowe Price, five-year horizon).
Lower-return / negative side: Invesco, Lazard
Lazard is underweight, arguing that China's apparent stability masks deeper weaknesses requiring meaningful government intervention. Invesco's 5.8% leans on its strongest currency tailwind to offset weak earnings growth and a negative buyback yield.
Leans on: Earnings growth, buyback yield (negative) and currency assumptions (Invesco); structural and policy assessment (Lazard).
What would settle it: Chinese earnings revisions, net share issuance, the yuan against the dollar and the scale of policy support.

Japan equity — Five overweights, mostly on governance reform, versus one underweight; public numbers 6.3-8.6%, median 6.4% (n=3).

directional divergence · public IQR 115bp, n=3 · stances ▲5 ■2 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: Invesco, Lazard, Goldman Sachs AM, Mercer, Schroders
Invesco (overweight, 8.6%, the range high) cites improved valuations, stable earnings growth and a positive currency adjustment. Lazard and Mercer point to governance reform delivering results and improving profitability; GSAM adds Takaichi fiscal support, wage-led spending, buybacks and a retail shift into risk assets. Schroders is also overweight.
Leans on: Governance and profitability trends, buybacks, starting valuations, yen appreciation via currency adjustment, fiscal and wage dynamics.
Lower-return / negative side: BNY Investments, Amundi
Amundi is underweight, arguing the strong rally has stretched valuations despite solid fundamentals. BNY's 6.3%, just under the 6.4% median (n=3), reflects a lower real growth outlook only partly offset by an assumed yen appreciation.
Leans on: Post-rally valuations (Amundi); real growth outlook and yen appreciation assumption (BNY).
What would settle it: Japanese ROE and buyback follow-through versus post-rally multiples, and USD/JPY, since Invesco's and BNY's numbers both lean on yen appreciation.

EM equity — Seven overweights against Vanguard's 3.0% outlier; other public numbers cluster at 7.1-8.8%.

directional divergence · public IQR 122bp, n=6 · stances ▲7 ■2 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: Robeco, T. Rowe Price, BNY Investments, Goldman Sachs AM, BlackRock Investment Institute, Schroders, Amundi
Robeco's 8.8% (five-year) makes EM its top asset class on trade rewiring; T. Rowe Price's 8.7% follows improving earnings momentum; BNY's 8.1% (overweight) cites growth, AI and dollar weakness. GSAM and BII add strong earnings, cheaper valuations and a softer dollar. Schroders and Amundi are overweight on stance.
Leans on: EM earnings momentum and growth premium, P/E discount to the US, dollar path, AI-related exposure, trade realignment.
Lower-return / negative side: Vanguard, Invesco, Fidelity International
Vanguard (underweight) has the range low, 3.0%: the strong EM rally lifted valuations, making EM its weakest equity forecast with the highest volatility. Invesco's 7.1% (neutral) sees a smaller valuation drag and better currency contribution, still below developed ex-US; Fidelity's 8.0% sits just under the median despite citing faster growth and cheaper valuations.
Leans on: Post-rally starting valuations and volatility (Vanguard); valuation drag and currency contribution (Invesco); growth and valuation inputs (Fidelity).
What would settle it: EM forward earnings revisions against the post-rally re-rating, and the dollar's direction; Vanguard's gap to peers is a starting-valuation dispute.

US IG corporate — US IG: five neutrals on tight spreads; public numbers 4.1-5.5% hinge on spread-widening versus carry assumptions.

directional divergence · public IQR 60bp, n=5 · stances ▲2 ■5 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: Invesco, Fidelity International, Goldman Sachs AM, Schroders
Invesco's 5.5%, the range high, rests on a higher total yield than Treasuries with limited credit losses; Fidelity's 5.4% combines rates income and spread carry, though it stays neutral given tight spreads. GSAM is overweight on mid-cycle fundamentals, value in segments such as banks and rate cuts lifting total returns. Schroders is also overweight.
Leans on: All-in yield over Treasuries, spread carry net of expected defaults and downgrades, mid-cycle credit fundamentals, expected rate cuts (GSAM).
Lower-return / negative side: Vanguard, T. Rowe Price
Vanguard is underweight: rich valuations leave its credit index (IG corporate plus government-related) with no forecast premium over Treasuries, at 4.8%. T. Rowe Price's 4.1% (five-year), the range low, reflects tight starting spreads and expected modest widening. Both sit below the 5.2% median.
Leans on: Starting spreads near historical tights, expected spread widening, forecast premium of credit over the Treasury index.
What would settle it: IG spreads relative to historical tights and issuance volume; widening would back the low-end forecasts, stable spreads the carry-based ones.

US high yield — Three overweights versus three underweights; carry from high yields against PIMCO's credit-loss-cycle warning.

directional divergence · public IQR 90bp, n=7 · stances ▲3 ■2 ▼3
Higher-return / positive side: Invesco, BNY Investments, Fidelity International, Goldman Sachs AM, Schroders
Invesco and BNY (both 6.1%, the range high) start from high all-in yields and still net out credit losses and expected spread widening; BNY is neutral on stance. Fidelity's 5.9% balances higher income against losses and tight spreads. GSAM is overweight on strong credit metrics, contained defaults, a better ratings mix and supportive M&A. Schroders is also overweight.
Leans on: All-in yield, expected default and recovery losses, assumed spread widening (Invesco, BNY), credit metrics and ratings mix (GSAM).
Lower-return / negative side: State Street, AQR, Vanguard, PIMCO, Amundi
PIMCO is underweight with high conviction: a credit loss cycle is beginning and tight spreads reflect complacency, notably in lower-quality, levered credit. Amundi, underweight, notes spreads near historic tights. Vanguard (5.2%, underweight) sees only a modest premium over Treasuries for higher volatility; State Street's 4.6%, the range low, models spread term structure and defaults; AQR is at 5.0%.
Leans on: Spreads near historical tights, default-cycle onset (PIMCO), spread term structure and default probabilities (State Street), premium versus Treasuries per unit of volatility.
What would settle it: Default and recovery rates among lower-rated, levered issuers and the direction of HY spreads; US recession in 2026 is priced at 3.5% (Kalshi) and 6.5% (Polymarket).

Commodities — Widest IQR of any row (225bp), with estimates split into 2.3-3.8% and 5.7-6.7% clusters.

numeric divergence · public IQR 225bp, n=7 · stances ▲1 ■1 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: AQR, Robeco, Vanguard
AQR (6.7%, the range high, overweight) adds a long-run diversified-futures excess return, measured over more than a century, to real cash. Robeco's 5.8% (five-year) rests on geopolitical supply disruptions and dollar dynamics. Vanguard's 5.7% on the Bloomberg Commodity Index comes without specific commentary, though it flags higher energy prices as an inflation risk.
Leans on: Long-history futures excess return plus real cash yield (AQR); geopolitical supply shocks and the dollar (Robeco); energy-led inflation risk (Vanguard).
Lower-return / negative side: BNY Investments, State Street, Invesco
BNY's 2.3%, the range low, is roughly unchanged from last year, with little stated rationale. State Street's 3.2% builds from US inflation and dollar forecasts, long-term roll return and collateral return. Invesco's 3.8% is its lowest expected return of any asset class, framed as a diversifying return driver after a slight quarter-on-quarter improvement.
Leans on: Building blocks: collateral (cash) return, long-term roll return, US inflation and dollar forecasts (State Street); limited published rationale from BNY and Invesco.
What would settle it: Roll yield (futures curve shape), collateral cash rates and whether energy-driven inflation persists; this site's LTCMA (3.5%) sits with the lower cluster.

Bank loans — Two public numbers (6.0%, 6.9%); GSAM's rate-relief overweight against PIMCO's leveraged-loss underweight.

directional divergence · public IQR 45bp, n=2 · stances ▲1 ■0 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: Invesco, Goldman Sachs AM
GSAM is overweight: lower policy rates would ease interest burdens for floating-rate leveraged-loan issuers and improve coverage. Invesco's 6.9%, the higher of the two public numbers, starts from a high floating-rate yield partly offset by expected credit losses.
Leans on: Floating-rate all-in yield, expected credit losses, assumed policy-rate cuts improving interest coverage (GSAM).
Lower-return / negative side: T. Rowe Price, PIMCO
PIMCO is underweight, expecting significantly higher losses in leveraged lending where floating-rate structures meet high leverage. T. Rowe Price's 6.0% (five-year) is the lower public number, though T. Rowe Price still ranks loans highest among public US fixed income on floating-rate carry with little duration.
Leans on: Leverage and floating-rate debt-service burden, expected loss rates (PIMCO); carry versus duration exposure (T. Rowe Price).
What would settle it: Leveraged-loan default and recovery rates and interest coverage; GSAM's relief case needs cuts, while zero 2026 Fed cuts are priced at 95.8% (Kalshi) and 95.9% (Polymarket).

Private equity — Highest median at 9.7% (n=5), but estimates span 6.6-10.5% on leverage, fee and premium assumptions.

numeric divergence · public IQR 155bp, n=5 · stances ▲5 ■1 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: BNY Investments, T. Rowe Price, Amundi, BlackRock Investment Institute
BNY's 10.5%, the range high, was raised sharply on the illiquidity premium and private capital's role funding AI and infrastructure. T. Rowe Price's 9.8% (five-year) rests on an illiquidity premium over public equity. Amundi is overweight on an illiquidity premium net of fees but flags slower distributions. BII's overweight covers venture capital only.
Leans on: Illiquidity premium over listed equity net of fees; private capital's role in AI and infrastructure financing (BNY).
Lower-return / negative side: AQR, State Street
AQR's 6.6%, the range low, comes from a levered dividend-discount model net of fees, even after lower debt costs and multiple expansion lifted its estimate. State Street's 8.2% leverage-adjusts historical PE performance against listed equities, net of fees. Both sit below the 9.7% median.
Leans on: Levered dividend-discount model with explicit fee drag (AQR); leverage-adjusted historical PE-versus-listed performance (State Street).
What would settle it: Realized net-of-fee PE returns versus listed equity, distribution pace, exit multiples and leveraged-financing costs.

Private credit — Four overweights versus PIMCO's high-conviction underweight; public numbers 5.0-9.2%, median 6.6% (n=4).

directional divergence · public IQR 143bp, n=4 · stances ▲4 ■2 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: T. Rowe Price, Fidelity International, Goldman Sachs AM, BlackRock Investment Institute, Amundi
T. Rowe Price's 9.2% (five-year), the range high, cites an illiquidity premium and structural demand for private capital; Fidelity's 6.9% (neutral) relies on floating income. GSAM sees yields above public markets with lower historical defaults; BII prefers credit with clear cash flows and lender protections. Amundi sees an attractive risk-adjusted alternative to liquid credit.
Leans on: Illiquidity premium, floating-rate yields net of fees and losses, historical default experience, structural demand for private capital.
Lower-return / negative side: AQR, State Street, PIMCO
PIMCO is underweight with high conviction, citing emerging stress in private corporate credit and middle-market direct lending: maturity extensions, PIK use and financial engineering. AQR's 5.0%, the range low, models it as levered floating-rate high yield, cut by narrower spreads and lower cash rates; State Street's 6.4% covers senior direct lending net of fees.
Leans on: Credit-stress indicators (PIK usage, maturity extensions); levered high-yield proxy with current spreads and cash rates (AQR); senior direct-lending premium (State Street).
What would settle it: PIK share, non-accruals and amend-and-extend activity in middle-market direct lending, plus realized defaults and recoveries versus public high yield.

Method. Documents are collected monthly from the publishers' public pages. Each one is read into a fixed schema by a language model that must quote every number verbatim. A number that cannot be found in the document text is dropped. Ranges become midpoints. Real forecasts become nominal using the publisher's own inflation assumption. Arithmetic averages become geometric with the publisher's own volatility (g = a − σ²/2), so every figure compounds the same way. Proxies are tagged on hover (e.g. a Eurozone index in the Europe row, a total-market index in US large cap). Horizons and currencies are never adjusted: they are labelled, and non-USD figures stay out of the consensus. Only publishers whose material may be republished appear with numbers; the rest appear as their stated views, with a link to the original. Prediction-market odds are read-only public prices. Figures are the publishers' own estimates, not this site's, and nothing here is investment advice.

Third-party forecasts are attributed to their publishers and linked to the originals. Not investment advice.
