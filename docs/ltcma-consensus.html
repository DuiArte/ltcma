Summary

  • Equity medians: EM 8.1 (n=6), ACWI 6.8 (n=5), US large cap 6.3 (n=7, IQR 90bp), US small cap 6.0 (n=6); EM's range runs from an outlying 3.0 to 8.8.
  • Bond medians ladder from cash 3.6 (n=6) and TIPS 3.8 (n=4) through US agg 4.6, intermediate Treasuries 4.8 (n=7), IG 5.2 and high yield 5.5 (n=7) to EM USD sovereigns 5.7 (n=8).
  • Alternatives: private equity's 9.7 (n=5) is the highest median of any row; private credit 6.6 (n=4), commodities 5.7 (n=7), hedge funds 5.5 (n=4).
  • Widest disagreements, ranked by IQR among rows with n≥4: commodities 195bp, US small cap 173bp, private equity 155bp, private credit 143bp, EM equity 123bp. Tightest: TIPS 32bp, EM local debt 41bp.
  • This site's LTCMA differs most from the public median on commodities: 3.5 vs 5.7 (n=7), 220bp lower. Next come UK equity (7.0 vs 9.1) and gold (3.3 vs 1.6), resting on n=2 and n=1.
  • Other gaps on n≥4 rows: this site's LTCMA exceeds the median for TIPS (4.5 vs 3.8) and trails for high yield (5.0 vs 5.5) and EM local debt (4.8 vs 5.3).
  • Fed: next-FOMC hold priced at 85.5-87.5%, a 25bp hike at 12.5-13.5%; zero cuts this year at 95.5-96.9%. That fits the higher-for-longer calls of Vanguard, Invesco and BlackRock Investment Institute.
  • Easing calls (Goldman Sachs AM, UBS GWM CIO) lack market support: exactly two cuts this year prices at 0.5-0.7%. US recession odds of 3.5% (Kalshi) and 7.5% (Polymarket) fit the mid-cycle views.

Material divergences

Rows where the publishers materially disagree: public IQR >= 150bp with n >= 4, or >= 1 overweight and >= 1 underweight stance. Arguments are paraphrased from the publishers' own documents.

US large cap — AI earnings versus valuation; underweights outnumber overweights; 5.1-7.6 range around a 6.3 median (n=7).

directional divergence · public IQR 90bp, n=7 · stances ▲1 ■1 ▼3
Higher-return / positive side: BNY Investments, T. Rowe Price, Fidelity International, Goldman Sachs AM
AI-driven earnings growth carries most of the case. BNY Investments (7.6, range high) holds valuations constant and adds AI-boosted earnings-share growth; Fidelity International (6.4) sees AI-lifted revenue growth outweighing above-equilibrium margins. T. Rowe Price (6.4) sits just above the median despite flagging elevated growth-stock valuations. Goldman Sachs AM, overweight, cites mega-cap earnings power and durable AI capex.
Leans on: Income, inflation, real GDP and AI-boosted earnings-share growth at constant valuations (BNY); AI-driven revenue growth, dividends, buybacks less margin mean-reversion (Fidelity); mega-cap earnings and AI capex (GSAM).
Lower-return / negative side: Vanguard, State Street, Invesco, PIMCO, Lazard, Amundi
Valuation is the binding constraint. Vanguard (5.1, range low) notes multiples climbed further after the recent rally; State Street and Invesco (both 5.5) let valuation drag offset income and earnings growth. PIMCO sees a near-zero equity risk premium but calls no correction; Lazard expects allocators to rotate away as US resilience looks doubtful; Amundi is underweight.
Leans on: Elevated starting valuations and an explicit valuation-drag term (Vanguard, Invesco, State Street); compressed US equity risk premium (PIMCO); doubts over underlying US economic resilience (Lazard).
What would settle it: Whether AI-driven earnings growth keeps pace with multiples: mega-cap forward earnings revisions versus P/E expansion, and the equity earnings yield versus real Treasury yields.

US small cap — IQR of 173bp, widest of any equity row, around a 6.0 median (n=6).

numeric divergence · public IQR 173bp, n=6 · stances ▲1 ■0 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: AQR, BNY Investments, T. Rowe Price, Goldman Sachs AM
AQR (7.4) and BNY Investments (7.5, range high) anchor the top, although BNY builds in only a weak size premium because companies stay private longer; T. Rowe Price (6.3) selectively upgraded the segment with a valuation-anchored forecast. Goldman Sachs AM, overweight, cites attractive valuations, expected rate cuts, accelerating earnings and a dealmaking revival.
Leans on: AQR: small-cap local real return plus expected US inflation; BNY: factor model with weak size premium; T. Rowe: valuation anchor; GSAM: valuations, rate-cut path, earnings momentum, M&A.
Lower-return / negative side: Vanguard, Invesco, State Street
Post-rally valuations and weaker long-run earnings growth cap returns. Vanguard (5.7) says the strong small-cap rally lifted valuations, cutting expected returns about as much as for large caps; Invesco (5.3) sees lower long-term earnings growth keeping small caps behind mid caps despite a smaller valuation drag; State Street sits at the 5.1 range low and expects higher volatility.
Leans on: Post-rally starting valuations (Vanguard); long-term earnings-growth assumptions and a shrinking valuation drag (Invesco); higher expected volatility (State Street).
What would settle it: Small- versus large-cap realized earnings growth and relative valuations, plus the Fed path: zero cuts this year priced at 95.5% (Kalshi) and 96.9% (Polymarket).

US high yield — (median 5.5, n=7): carry versus default losses and spread widening from tight levels.

directional divergence · public IQR 80bp, n=7 · stances ▲1 ■0 ▼2
Higher-return / positive side: BNY Investments, Invesco, Fidelity International, Goldman Sachs AM
High starting yields dominate. Invesco and Fidelity International expect carry to outrun credit losses even with spreads near historic tights; BNY Investments (6.1, range high) nets defaults from yield while still assuming spreads widen back toward their long-run median. Goldman Sachs AM, overweight, cites strong interest coverage, contained defaults, a better ratings mix and supportive M&A.
Leans on: Starting total yield and spread income minus expected default losses and spread drag (Invesco, Fidelity, BNY); interest coverage, defaults, ratings mix and M&A (GSAM).
Lower-return / negative side: State Street, AQR, Vanguard, PIMCO, Amundi
Spreads pay too little for the risk. Vanguard (5.2) sees rich credit valuations offering limited compensation; State Street (4.6, range low) gets only a modest premium over IG after default probabilities; AQR (5.0) also sits below. PIMCO, underweight, calls tight spreads complacent as defaults rise and AI disrupts levered old-economy issuers; Amundi is underweight.
Leans on: Rich credit valuations and tight spreads (Vanguard, PIMCO); spread term structure and default probabilities (State Street); real return plus expected inflation (AQR); rising defaults (PIMCO).
What would settle it: Realized default and recovery rates versus forecast losses, whether spreads widen from near-historic tights, and US recession odds this year: 3.5% (Kalshi), 7.5% (Polymarket).

Commodities — Widest IQR of any row (195bp); forecasts span 2.3-6.7 around a 5.7 median (n=7).

numeric divergence · public IQR 195bp, n=7 · stances ▲0 ■1 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: AQR, Invesco, Robeco
Structural premia and inflation sensitivity. AQR (6.7, range high) adds a long-run excess return of equal-weighted futures over cash to the expected real cash return; Invesco (6.5) cites direct sensitivity to inflation and supply shocks as a differentiated return driver; Robeco, also above the median on a shorter horizon, says outcomes hinge on conflicts and the US dollar.
Leans on: Long-run excess return of equal-weighted commodity futures over cash plus real cash yield (AQR); inflation and supply-shock sensitivity (Invesco); conflict risk and dollar path (Robeco).
Lower-return / negative side: BNY Investments, State Street, T. Rowe Price
Low numbers, often with thin disclosure. State Street (3.2) builds the return from US inflation and dollar forecasts, a long-term roll return and collateral return; BNY Investments (2.3, range low) expects a low return with high volatility, little changed from its prior edition, without stating drivers; T. Rowe Price (5.2) gives no asset-specific rationale.
Leans on: US inflation and dollar forecasts, long-term roll return and collateral return (State Street); undisclosed drivers for BNY Investments and T. Rowe Price's Bloomberg Commodity Index forecast.
What would settle it: Realized roll and collateral returns on broad commodity futures indices, the dollar trend, and supply shocks from conflicts.

Global govt bonds — Numbers cluster tightly (IQR 23bp, n=3); stances split on fiscal risk versus diversification value.

directional divergence · public IQR 23bp, n=3 · stances ▲2 ■1 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: Robeco, PIMCO
Higher starting yields lift prospective returns, and divergent policy cycles open country and curve opportunities. Robeco (5.3, range high; USD-hedged, shorter horizon) credits higher yields but cautions they carry their own risks. PIMCO, overweight with high conviction, sees non-US sovereigns diversifying against US fiscal unsustainability.
Leans on: Starting yields on USD-hedged developed sovereigns (Robeco); cross-country divergence in cycles and policy, plus the US fiscal trajectory (PIMCO).
Lower-return / negative side: Fidelity International, BNY Investments
BNY Investments, underweight, argues fiscal strain erodes sovereigns' risk-adjusted appeal and sees real assets and private markets as alternative diversifiers. Fidelity International sits at the 4.8 range low, below the median, although its own stance is overweight: it treats income as the main return driver and expects sovereigns to diversify in recessionary shocks.
Leans on: Fiscal strain and sovereign risk-adjusted returns (BNY Investments); government yield-curve income and recession-hedging behavior (Fidelity International).
What would settle it: Term premia and long-end yields in deficit-heavy markets, and whether developed sovereigns rally, rather than sell off, during the next growth scare.

Private equity — Illiquidity premium versus fee and leverage drag; range 6.6-10.5 around a 9.7 median (n=5).

numeric divergence · public IQR 155bp, n=5 · stances ▲3 ■0 ▼0
Higher-return / positive side: BNY Investments, T. Rowe Price, BlackRock Investment Institute
A strong illiquidity premium on top of public-equity factor exposure. BNY Investments (10.5, range high), overweight, adds private capital's role in funding AI-driven innovation and infrastructure; T. Rowe Price, above the median on a shorter horizon, cites the illiquidity premium and active management. BlackRock Investment Institute, overweight with high conviction, names venture capital among alpha opportunities.
Leans on: Public-equity factor exposure plus an illiquidity premium (BNY Investments); Cambridge Associates global PE index forecast (T. Rowe Price); macro-regime alpha view on venture (BlackRock Investment Institute).
Lower-return / negative side: AQR, State Street
Explicit fee and leverage adjustments leave less excess return. AQR (6.6, range low) values US buyouts with a levered dividend discount model net of fees, and sees private-asset premia compressed against positive real cash and bond yields; State Street, also below the median, adjusts historical PE performance versus listed equities for leverage, net of fees.
Leans on: Levered dividend discount model for US buyouts with debt cost, multiple expansion and fee inputs (AQR); leverage-adjusted historical PE-versus-listed performance, net of fees (State Street).
What would settle it: Buyout exit multiples and distributions versus public-market equivalents, buyout financing costs, and deployment of the elevated dry powder Fidelity International flags.

Private credit — Yield pickup versus loosening underwriting; range 5.0-9.2 around a 6.6 median (n=4).

directional divergence · public IQR 143bp, n=4 · stances ▲4 ■0 ▼1
Higher-return / positive side: T. Rowe Price, Fidelity International, Goldman Sachs AM, BlackRock Investment Institute, Amundi
Private loans still out-yield public credit with historically lower defaults than syndicated loans (Goldman Sachs AM, overweight). T. Rowe Price (9.2, range high, shorter horizon) cites the illiquidity premium and structural demand for private financing; Fidelity International, overweight, sees higher-rate income and no immediate stress. BlackRock Investment Institute names selected private credit an alpha opportunity; Amundi is overweight.
Leans on: Cliffwater Direct Lending index forecast with illiquidity premium (T. Rowe Price); net-of-fee direct-lending income at higher base rates (Fidelity International); yield pickup and default history versus syndicated loans (GSAM).
Lower-return / negative side: AQR, State Street, PIMCO
AQR (5.0, range low) treats private credit as levered floating-rate high yield whose fees offset any illiquidity premium, with narrower spreads and lower cash rates cutting returns; State Street (6.4) sits just below the median on a net-of-fee senior direct-lending premium. PIMCO, underweight with high conviction, reads loose underwriting, PIK and maturity extensions as signs of real defaults.
Leans on: Floating-rate high-yield proxy with leverage, fees, spreads and cash rates (AQR); senior direct-lending premium over public credit (State Street); underwriting quality, PIK use and maturity extensions (PIMCO).
What would settle it: Non-accrual and PIK-income shares in direct-lending portfolios, realized loss rates versus syndicated loans, and the base-rate path that drives floating-rate income.

Method. Documents are collected monthly from the publishers' public pages. Each one is read into a fixed schema by a language model that must quote every number verbatim. A number that cannot be found in the document text is dropped. Ranges become midpoints. Real forecasts become nominal using the publisher's own inflation assumption. Arithmetic averages become geometric with the publisher's own volatility (g = a − σ²/2), so every figure compounds the same way. Proxies are tagged on hover (e.g. a Eurozone index in the Europe row, a total-market index in US large cap). Horizons and currencies are never adjusted: they are labelled, and non-USD figures stay out of the consensus. Only publishers whose material may be republished appear with numbers; the rest appear as their stated views, with a link to the original. Prediction-market odds are read-only public prices. Figures are the publishers' own estimates, not this site's, and nothing here is investment advice.

Third-party forecasts are attributed to their publishers and linked to the originals. Not investment advice.
